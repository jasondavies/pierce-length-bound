# Joint integer invariants for the Pierce remainder iteration

## Status and statement

Research proof candidate, 19 October 2026. This note extends
`pierce_151_513_proof_candidate.md`, retaining the finite-moment,
minor-pattern, noncollision, conic, and line-exclusion framework specified
below. It is not an externally reviewed or formally verified proof. No
priority claim is made. Exact computations check the new algebra; they do
not prove the asymptotic estimates.

Subject to that retained framework, the new bounds are

\[
P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{128/435+\varepsilon},
\qquad 128/435=0.2942528735632184\ldots,
\]

and, on optimizing powers of the same coordinates,

\[
\boxed{
P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{\alpha_*+\varepsilon},
\qquad
\alpha_* = \frac{1310-\sqrt{655}}{4365}
          =0.29425132467717309166\ldots.
}
\]

A concrete rational version of the optimized result is

\[
P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{2201/7480+\varepsilon}.
\]

The principal new improvement is the simple 128/435 bound; the additional
optimization is much smaller. Its savings over 151/513 and above 5/17 are

\[
151/513-128/435=7/74385,
\qquad 128/435-5/17=1/7395.
\]

The construction uses three small curvature minors and the small rank-five
Hankel invariant as extra integer coordinates. No new freely counted
coefficient pattern is introduced. The weighted-lattice enumeration lemma
is not needed. The same coefficient-uniform affine-curve theorem as in the
preceding note is the arithmetic point-counting input.

This does not resolve the conjectured O((log a)^2) bound.

## 1. Retained setup and exact dependencies

For integers a>b>0, write

\[
a=q_i b_i+b_{i+1},\qquad k_i=q_{i+1}-q_i>0,
\]

until the first zero remainder. The number of positive states is P(a,b).
Let T count states with Q <= q_i < 2Q. After discarding a bounded number of
endpoints, a fixed positive proportion of starts have their next sixteen
gaps bounded by H=C_0 Q/T. All local identities are applied only to such
starts; this supplies more than the finite support required at rank five.

Put, with suitable fixed positive constants,

\[
X=C_X aH^6/Q^4,\quad K=Q^4/(aH^5),\quad
S=C_S aH^3/Q^3,\quad Y=C_Y aH^{10}/Q^5.
\tag{1.1}
\]

X and S can be taken at least one for a represented start. The proof below
shows the same for Y. H>=1 in the selected windows. All these scales are
bounded by fixed powers of a.

We retain the following inputs from the 151/513 note and its explicitly
named dependencies.

1. There are finite positive functionals mu_i,nu_i with
   \[
   (q_i+x)\nu_i=\mu_i,\quad \mu_i(1)=\mu_i(x)=1,\quad
   \nu_i(1)=b_i/a.
   \]
   For local nodes s_j=q_(i+j)-q_i, s_0=0, and
   F_j(x)=product_(v<j)(x-s_v),
   \[
   \mu_i(F_j g)=\mu_{i+j}(g(s_j+x)),\qquad \mu_i(F_j)=1.
   \]
   Raw moments of order h have gap degree at most h-1 for h>=1, with the
   stated successor margin. In particular the moments through degree eight
   use only the first seven future gaps.

2. Define
   \[
   H_r(i)=\det[\mu_i(x^{u+v})]_{u,v<r},\qquad
   I_r(i)=a\det[\nu_i(x^{u+v})]_{u,v<r}.
   \]
   The I_r used here are positive integers, and
   \[
   I_r(i)\le aH_r(i)/q_i^r.
   \tag{1.2}
   \]
   Integrality follows from the rank-one nonintegral part of the weighted
   moment matrix; positivity is the positive Gram determinant statement.

3. With d_j=b_(i+j)-b_(i+j+1) and
   \[
   r_j=k_j(s_j+s_{j+1})+k_{j+1},
   \]
   form the 3-by-5 matrix with rows d,k,r. All ten maximal minors are
   bounded by X. There are O(X^5) possible complete minor vectors, including
   common factors. The matrix's first consecutive minor is
   \[
   A=p_{012}=I_4(i)>0.
   \]
   The next two are A',A''>0. The seven coordinates A,A',A'',U,V,U',V'
   determine the complete pattern and two quartics. Their cumulative
   coefficient vectors are
   \[
   (-\rho,u,v,1),\quad(-\rho',u',v',1),\qquad
   \rho>0,\ u>0,\ v<0.
   \]
   The first and fifth gaps are recovered linearly:
   \[
   k_0=(u k_1+v k_2+k_3)/\rho,
   \quad k_4=\rho' k_1-u' k_2-v' k_3.
   \tag{1.3}
   \]

4. For a fixed pattern the monic quartic P and the translated next quartic
   R give Z=xR. Actual states satisfy
   \[
   f=0,\qquad A\mathcal R=aD^4,\qquad
   \mathcal R=\operatorname{Res}(P,Z),
   \tag{1.4}
   \]
   in the three independent coordinates (k_1,k_2,k_3). The first polynomial
   f is an absolutely irreducible quadratic whose homogeneous quadratic
   part has rank three. D is the polynomial quartic norm, of gap degree at
   most three, after using the second quadratic to eliminate k_5. At actual
   states D=nu_i(P^2)>0. The intersection in (1.4) has total curve degree at
   most 24; f and A R-aD^4 have no common surface component.

5. The quartic Newton coefficients c_j are nonzero and have exactly four
   possible vanishing pair sums. Patterns satisfying any such pair sum are
   called exceptional. Their number is O(X^4 log(2X)). In the main range
   K>=C_1 X, no selected actual state lies on any line component, including
   exceptional patterns. In the complementary range, the drop bound gives
   exponent 5/17.

6. At every normalized branch above infinity of a nonexceptional component,
   with original hyperplane multiplicity e, the five node leading values
   z_0,...,z_4 are distinct. In particular e is positive and even, and the
   original infinity divisor has total degree equal to the curve degree.
   The resultant has exact pole order 10e at that branch.

7. The two quartics rationally reconstruct q and weighted moments through
   degree eight. Impose the actual moment equations through raw degree six
   and A P(-q)=aH_4. If any is not an identity on a component, it cuts out
   only O(1) actual points by bounded-degree Bezout. Otherwise, on that
   component, the rational function
   \[
   E_0=a\det[\nu(x^{r+s})]_{r,s<3}
   \]
   equals I_3 at actual states and has exact zero order 3e/2 at every old
   infinity branch. This is the resolvent and pole-order lemma of Sections
   2--4 of the 151/513 note. Its numerator and denominator have degrees at
   most 240, independently of all numerical data.

8. Nonexceptional conics contribute O_eta(a^eta) actual states per component.
   They have a unique double infinity point and an integer polynomial
   parameter t. E_0(t) is a proper nonzero rational function; integrality
   implies divisibility of its denominator value by a fixed resultant of
   polynomially bounded height. Section 7 of the preceding note gives the
   full argument. This conic lemma is unchanged by the new coordinates.

A curve irreducible over Q but not geometrically irreducible can be counted
separately by O(1) rational points here. Its rational points lie on the
intersection of two distinct conjugate geometric components. Their total
degrees are bounded by 24. Thus degree statements below may be proved on
geometrically irreducible components over an algebraic closure. For the
unbounded contributions the component itself is defined over Q.

These dependencies are proof statements, not empirical assumptions. This
note does not claim that a curve consists entirely of orbit points.
Additional actual-state identities are always cuts unless proved identical.

## 2. A second small integer coordinate: I_5

Define

\[
\mathcal J=A D.
\tag{2.1}
\]

At actual states this is exactly I_5(i), not a new coefficient parameter.
To verify it, write tau_r=det[nu_i(x^(u+v))]_(u,v<r). A monic Gram basis
change from 1,x,...,x^4 to 1,x,x^2,x^3,P block-diagonalizes the rank-five
matrix by orthogonality. Therefore

\[
\tau_5=\tau_4\nu_i(P^2).
\]

Since a tau_4=A and nu_i(P^2)=D,

\[
\boxed{I_5(i)=a\tau_5=A D=\mathcal J.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently mathcal J is a positive integer at each actual state. Since D
is a polynomial in the three gap variables, mathcal J has no poles above
any point of the original affine curve.

### 2.1 Uniform size bound

The raw determinant H_5 is O(H^10) on a selected window. This follows from
the Newton Gram basis: a mixed entry mu(F_j F_l) has gap degree at most
min(j,l), and only total degree at most 0+1+2+3+4=10 can occur in its
determinant.

Here is also an explicit positive bound. For h=1,2,3,4, respectively, the
raw moment at any needed shifted start satisfies

\[
\mu(x^h)\le B_h H^{h-1},\qquad(B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4)=(1,2,5,15).
\]

These constants follow directly from
mu_i(x^h)=mu_(i+1)((k_i+x)^(h-1)) and H>=1. Moreover

\[
\mu_i(F_j^2)=\mu_{i+j}(F_j(s_j+x))
\le\mu_{i+j}((jH+x)^j).
\]

For j=0,1,2,3,4 the last expression is at most M_j H^j, with
M=(1,2,10,77,799). Hadamard's inequality for the positive Gram matrix gives

\[
0<H_5\le (1\cdot2\cdot10\cdot77\cdot799)H^{10}
       =1230460 H^{10}.
\]

Rank-five Gram comparison now yields

\[
\boxed{1\le\mathcal J\ll aH^{10}/Q^5=Y/C_Y.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The raw moments here use at most the first seven gaps and therefore satisfy
the fixed sixteen-gap support convention.

### 2.2 Pole order

At a nonexceptional infinity branch, the resultant's nonzero leading
Vandermonde gives pole order 10e. Equation (1.4) therefore gives

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal J)=-5e/2.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

There is no ambiguity from roots: mathcal J is the already defined rational
function A D, and its order is an integer. The retained parity theorem
makes e even.

Thus E_0 vanishes at the old infinity points while mathcal J has poles
there. Adding both scalar coordinates already raises the graph degree
from at least 5d/2 to at least 4d. The next section gains one more degree on
nonconic components by retaining all three nearby curvature minors.

## 3. Three curvature coordinates of the same size

At an actual state set

\[
e_{01}=d_0k_1-d_1k_0,\quad
e_{02}=d_0k_2-d_2k_0,\quad
e_{12}=d_1k_2-d_2k_1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The first is I_3(i). One direct proof uses the rank-three weighted Gram
determinant: change its row basis to 1,q+x,x(q+x), its column basis to
F_0,F_1,F_2, and then take consecutive column differences. Multiplication
by a gives exactly d_0 k_1-d_1 k_0.

Write

\[
B_0=k_0r_1-k_1r_0=H_3(i),\qquad
B_1=k_1r_2-k_2r_1=H_3(i+1).
\]

In gaps these are the exact cubic polynomials

\[
B_0=k_0^2k_1+(k_0-1)k_1^2+k_0k_2,
\]
\[
B_1=k_1^2k_2+(k_1-1)k_2^2+k_1k_3.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define on a surviving component

\[
E_{12}=\frac{B_1E_0-Ak_1}{B_0},\qquad
E_{02}=\frac{k_0E_{12}+k_2E_0}{k_1},\qquad
W=(E_{12},-E_{02},E_0).
\tag{3.3}
\]

At actual states these are the corresponding integer curvature minors.
Indeed the determinant identity and the elementary cross-product identity
give

\[
B_1e_{01}-B_0e_{12}=Ak_1,
\quad k_1e_{02}=k_0e_{12}+k_2e_{01}.
\]

The denominators B_0 and k_1 are strictly positive at actual states.
Components where either is identically zero have no such states. All
functions have bounded degrees. Equations (3.3) identically imply

\[
\boxed{W\cdot(k_0,k_1,k_2)=0,\qquad W\cdot(r_0,r_1,r_2)=A.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

These relations hold as rational-function identities, not only at sampled
orbit points.

### 3.1 Uniform size at actual states

For j=0,...,3 use the exact identity

\[
\frac1{q+y}=\frac1q-\frac y{q^2}+
             \frac{y^2}{q^2(q+y)}.
\]

The positive-function model gives

\[
\frac{b_{i+j}}a=\frac1q-\frac{s_j+1}{q^2}+\epsilon_j,
\qquad
0\le\epsilon_j\le
\frac{s_j^2+2s_j+1+k_j}{q^3}\le\frac{17H^2}{q^3}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{d_j}a=\frac{k_j}{q^2}+
             O(34H^2/q^3),\qquad j=0,1,2.
\]

The leading terms cancel in every 2-by-2 minor in (3.1), yielding

\[
\boxed{|e_{jl}|\le68 aH^3/q^3\ll S.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This estimate is uniform even if individual gaps are very unequal. It does
not divide by a small gap. Thus W supplies three small integer coordinates
without enlarging the scale used for I_3 alone.

### 3.2 Orders at the old infinity points

At each old branch the leading Vandermonde coefficients in B_0 and B_1
are nonzero, so both have pole order 3e. The retained resolvent calculation
gives ord(E_0)=3e/2. In (3.3), B_1 E_0 has pole order 3e/2, whereas A k_1
has pole order e; these cannot cancel. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}(E_{12})=3e/2.
\]

The second equation in (3.3) gives ord(E_02)>=3e/2, possibly strictly larger
because its numerator can cancel. Thus

\[
\boxed{\min_j\operatorname{ord}(W_j)=3e/2.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

At a point above the original affine part, all r_j are regular. The second
identity (3.4) implies that the W_j cannot all have positive order there.
They may have poles; if none has a pole, at least one is nonzero.

## 4. A projective direction supplies one additional degree

Let C be a geometrically irreducible nonexceptional component of degree
d>=4 which survives the actual-state cuts. Work on its nonsingular
projective normalization. Write

\[
E=\sum_{p\mid\infty} e_p[p],\qquad \deg E=d.
\]

Let F be the pole envelope of W: its multiplicity at a point is the maximum
of zero and the pole orders of the three W_j. By (3.6), F is supported over
the original affine part and is disjoint from E.

We claim

\[
\boxed{\deg F\ge 3d/2+1.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

First, the projective direction [W_0:W_1:W_2] is nonconstant on C. Otherwise
W would be a scalar rational function times a fixed nonzero vector v. The
first identity (3.4) would place (k_0,k_1,k_2) in the fixed plane v dot k=0.
The transformation from (k_1,k_2,k_3) to (k_0,k_1,k_2) has determinant 1/rho,
which is nonzero. C would therefore lie in an affine plane in the original
three gap coordinates. But it is also contained in the irreducible
quadratic f, which contains no plane. Its plane section has degree at most
two, contradicting d>=4.

Choose a smooth normalized point p above the original affine part, away
from all poles. The W-vector is nonzero there. Nonconstancy of its
projective direction gives a constant linear combination

\[
L=\lambda_0W_0+\lambda_1W_1+\lambda_2W_2
\]

which vanishes at p but is not the zero rational function. By (3.6), it has
zeros at the old infinity divisor with multiplicities at least 3e_p/2,
as well as the additional zero at p. Its pole divisor is bounded by F.
Equality of zero and pole divisor degrees proves (4.1).

Equivalently, removing the common zero divisor 3E/2 from the W-system
produces a nonconstant projective map whose pullback of O(1) has positive
integral degree. The elementary linear-combination argument above avoids
needing its exact degree.

## 5. The joint graph has degree at least 4d+1

Add both types of invariant at once:

\[
\Gamma_C=\overline{
\{(k_1,k_2,k_3,W_0,W_1,W_2,\mathcal J): (k_1,k_2,k_3)\in C\}}
\subset\mathbb A^7.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Projection to the first three coordinates is birational. At old infinity,
the generic affine hyperplane of this graph has pole order 5e/2, supplied
by mathcal J; this strictly dominates the gaps, while the W_j vanish. At a
finite pole of W it has the corresponding envelope pole order in F;
mathcal J and all the gap coordinates are regular there. A generic constant
coefficient choice avoids cancellation at the finitely many relevant
points. There are no other poles.

The graph degree is consequently

\[
\deg\Gamma_C=5d/2+\deg F\ge4d+1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This uses the standard equality between graph degree and the pole degree
of a generic affine linear combination on a birational parametrization of
its normalized curve. Normalization and proper birational maps preserve
hyperplane degree, including on singular curves. References are recorded
in Section 11.

An original quartic component therefore has graph degree at least 17.
Without using all of W, the scalar pair (E_0,mathcal J) gives the simpler
bound 4d and graph degree at least 16. This alone leads to 241/819; (4.1)
provides the stronger unpowered exponent 128/435.

All graph degrees have a uniform absolute upper bound. In the preceding
reconstruction E_0 has numerator and denominator degrees at most 240.
B_0,B_1 have degree three and each k_j degree one. A common denominator for
W has degree at most 244, and its numerators also have degrees at most 244.
Since mathcal J is polynomial of degree at most three, a generic graph
hyperplane clears to a polynomial of degree at most 247 in the original
variables. Thus deg Gamma_C <=247d is a sufficient upper bound.

All actual graph coordinates are integers, of sizes at most a constant
times

\[
\boxed{\max(H,S,Y).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The constants in (1.1) can absorb the fixed numerical bounds in Section 2
and Section 3. No integrality is asserted for arbitrary complex points of
the algebraic component.

## 6. Powered coordinates and a slightly better balance

For fixed positive integers m,n, keep the three original gap coordinates
and replace the other coordinates by

\[
W_0^m,\ W_1^m,\ W_2^m,\ \mathcal J^n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

They are still integers at actual states. Keeping the original gaps makes
the graph birational even when the powers lose information individually.
The old infinity pole degree is (5n/2)d and the finite pole envelope is mF.
Thus

\[
\boxed{\deg\Gamma_{C;m,n}\ge(5n+3m)d/2+m.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

For d>=4 this is at least

\[
\boxed{L=10n+7m.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The upper graph degree is O((m+n)d), with an absolute implied constant,
by clearing the same bounded-degree rational denominators. The coordinate
box radius is

\[
B\ll_{m,n}\max(H,S^m,Y^n).
\tag{6.4}
\]

The integers m,n are chosen independently of a,Q,T and of the pattern.
They may depend on a prescribed final epsilon; the theorem allows its
constant to depend on epsilon.

## 7. The local integer-point count

The external arithmetic input is the coefficient-uniform affine-curve
bound: for an affine curve over Q irreducible over Q, of degree delta, in
fixed ambient dimension N>=2,

\[
N_{\rm aff}(C,B)\ll_N \delta^3 B^{1/\delta}(\log B+\delta).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Use N=7 and the degree bounds of Section 6. For fixed m,n, the degrees are
bounded above and at least L. Every surviving nonexceptional component of
original degree at least four contributes at most

\[
O_{m,n}\bigl(\max(H,S^m,Y^n)^{1/L}\log(2\max(H,S^m,Y^n))\bigr).
\]

Fixed pattern and five gaps represent at most four orbit states: the
normalization A P(-q_i)=aH_4 is a nonzero quartic equation in q_i, and
quotients strictly increase. Alternatively, the full rational reconstruction
identifies the quotient uniquely. Both bounds suffice.

The number of patterns is O(X^5), and the number and total original degrees
of the components per pattern are bounded by 24. Thus the higher-degree
contribution is bounded by the displayed expression times O(X^5).

The unchanged remaining contributions are:

* O_eta(a^eta X^5) from nonexceptional conics and bounded-point cuts. For a
  parabola its integer parameter t gives a proper rational E_0(t), whose
  denominator values divide a fixed polynomial resultant at integral
  values. The resulting divisor count is uniform because all degrees are
  fixed and all heights are bounded by a fixed power of a.
* O_eta(a^eta X^4 H^(1/2)) from exceptional patterns when K>=C_1 X. There
  are O(X^4 log(2X)) such patterns; no line component contributes an actual
  selected state in this range, and the ordinary degree-two curve bound
  suffices for the remaining components. This overcounts exceptional
  patterns not supporting such components, which is harmless.

Logarithms can be absorbed into a^eta since H,S,Y,X are bounded by fixed
powers of a. We have proved, in the main range,

\[
\boxed{
T\ll_{m,n,\eta} a^\eta
\left[X^5+X^4H^{1/2}
+X^5\max(H,S^m,Y^n)^{1/(10n+7m)}\right].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This count does not use a weighted-lattice incidence bound, and neither
mathcal J nor any of the three W-coordinates is separately counted as a
pattern parameter.

## 8. Global exponents with m=n=1

The elementary drop estimate says, for every eta>0,

\[
Q\ll_\eta a^{1+\eta}/T^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

The total drop of the block is at most a/Q, so at least half are at most
2a/(QT). The identity a+d_i=(q_i+1)b_i and the distinctness of the
remainders bound their number by a short sum of tau(a+d). The elementary
estimate tau(N)<<_eta N^eta proves (8.1).

The scales satisfy

\[
H\asymp Q/T,\quad X\asymp aQ^2/T^6,\quad
S\asymp a/T^3,\quad Y\asymp aQ^5/T^{10}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

A fixed positive fraction of good windows exists: the block's internal
gaps sum to less than Q. Set H=C_0Q/T; at most T/C_0 gaps exceed H and each
spoils at most sixteen starts. Discard those starts and the last sixteen.
For sufficiently large fixed C_0 and T, at least T/2 starts remain. Bounded
T is harmless.

For m=n=1 we have L=17. Treat the possible largest terms in (7.2).

* X^5 gives T^51 <<_eta a^(15+11eta), with exponent 5/17.
* X^4 H^(1/2) gives T^85 <<_eta a^(25+19eta), also with exponent 5/17.
* X^5 H^(1/17) gives T^528 << a^(85+17eta) Q^171 and hence
  T^870 <<_eta a^(256+188eta).
* X^5 S^(1/17) gives T^530 << a^(86+17eta) Q^170 and hence
  T^870 <<_eta a^(256+187eta).
* X^5 Y^(1/17) gives T^537 << a^(86+17eta) Q^175 and hence
  T^887 <<_eta a^(261+192eta).

Here the same arbitrarily small eta can be selected before all uses; its
particular coefficients are immaterial for the final +epsilon statement.
The limiting exponent is

\[
256/870=128/435,
\quad
128/435-261/887=1/385845>0.
\]

The complementary range K<C_1X implies

\[
T^{11}\ll a^2Q^3,
\qquad T^{17}\ll_\eta a^{5+3\eta},
\]

with exponent 5/17, smaller by 1/7395 than 128/435.

Choose eta sufficiently small relative to the requested epsilon and sum
the O(log a) dyadic quotient blocks. This proves the unpowered candidate
128/435+epsilon.

At its critical balance, with common denominator 435,

\[
T\sim a^{128/435},\quad Q\sim a^{179/435},\quad
H\sim S\sim a^{51/435},\quad
Y\sim a^{50/435},\quad X\sim a^{25/435},\quad K\sim a^{26/435}.
\]

Thus the I_5 coordinate really is smaller than the curvature-coordinate
scale at this balance. The Y case was nevertheless included explicitly
rather than discarded on the basis of a heuristic balance.

## 9. Optimize the powered coordinates

Let L=10n+7m. The H term is no worse than the S term: either use (8.1) to
bound H by a^eta S and S>=1, or compute its exponent separately and use
m>=1. The two limiting exponents from the lifted term are

\[
E_S(m,n)=\frac{15L+m}{51L+3m},\qquad
E_Y(m,n)=\frac{15L+6n}{51L+20n}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

For example, substitute X^5 S^(m/L) to obtain
T^(31L+3m)<<a^(5L+m+O(eta))Q^(10L), and then use (8.1).
The Y term gives T^(31L+10n)<<a^(5L+n+O(eta))Q^(10L+5n).
All other ranges still have exponent 5/17. Both numbers in (9.1) are
strictly greater than 5/17, for m,n>0.

Put r=n/m. Then

\[
E_S(r)=\frac{150r+106}{510r+360},\qquad
E_Y(r)=\frac{156r+105}{530r+357}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Their derivatives are respectively

\[
-\frac{60}{(510r+360)^2}<0,
\qquad
\frac{42}{(530r+357)^2}>0.
\]

The maximum is minimized at their unique positive crossing. Equating them
gives

\[
30r^2-10r-21=0,
\qquad r_*=(5+\sqrt{655})/30.
\]

The common value is

\[
\boxed{\alpha_*=(1310-\sqrt{655})/4365.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Equivalently alpha_* is the smaller root of

\[
4365\alpha^2-2620\alpha+393=0.
\]

Positive rational ratios n/m approximate r_* arbitrarily closely. For a
prescribed epsilon, choose fixed positive integers m,n so that the maximum
in (9.2) is below alpha_*+epsilon/3. Choose the divisor and logarithm loss
eta sufficiently small after making this choice, and then absorb the final
dyadic logarithm. The graph degrees and constants may depend on epsilon,
which is allowed by the theorem. The integers m,n never depend on a or on
the orbit.

This proves the candidate alpha_*+epsilon bound. An explicit choice
m=50,n=51 has L=860 and yields

\[

Thus 2201/7480+epsilon is an entirely rational version. The improvement of

## 10. Verification and limits

The accompanying executable checks perform the following:

1. Seven generic symbolic wedge, cofactor, and invertible-coordinate
   identities, plus five generic orthogonality/norm identities.
2. Fresh exact arithmetic on 225 actual starts: 205 revisited inputs from
   the prior package and 20 additional deterministic random inputs. All
   have at least ten positive states. The checks recompute the finite
   measures, rank-three and rank-five weighted determinants, all three
   curvature minors, reconstruction equations (3.3), both dot products
   (3.4), the explicit curvature bound, I_5=A D, its Gram upper bound,
   the raw rank-five bound, and A P(-q)=aH_4. There are 4050 assertions.
3. Two synthetic birational degree-four curves, one with one old infinity
   point and one with two, whose joint graphs have degree exactly seventeen.
   These are not claimed to come from Pierce orbits. A further powered
   example has degree 44 for m=2,n=3, matching 10n+7m.
4. Exact optimization identities, rational exponent comparisons, and the
   critical-scale arithmetic.

The counts and summaries are recorded in results_actual.json and
results_symbolic.json. The checks do not verify divisor-degree or
coefficient-uniform counting theorems by sampling. The uniform reasoning
is in Sections 2--9. No independent-agent proof audit, external review,
or formal verification was performed for this refinement.

The strongest unpowered statement uses only deg F>=3d/2+1. This +1 is the
minimal contribution forced by the nonconstant projective curvature map.
A stronger lower bound for that map's degree, or a smaller collection of
realizable coefficient patterns, could improve the exponent further.
No stronger bound on that map is asserted here. The retained X^5,
exceptional-pattern, and complementary-scale terms still give the threshold
5/17 for this framework.
