New checked components
----------------------
ConicIntegerValues:
- For integral F,G coprime over Q with nonconstant G, any finite set of integer
  parameters satisfying G(t) | F(t) has cardinality at most
    2 deg(G) tau(|Res(F,G)|).
- The proof uses the integral resultant Bezout identity, signed divisors and
  polynomial root counting. It proves the resultant is nonzero from rational
  coprimality. Parameter-height assumptions are unnecessary for this exact bound.

ConicReconstruction:
- Proves independence of the shifted quartic/quintic rows via coprimality.
- Proves positive variance and both moment normalizations for actual supported
  orbit states, then the formulas
    q = (m1-m2)/(m1-1), b = a/(q+m1).
- Proves the relevant denominator is nonzero. The full cofactor construction,
  its rank statement and uniform parameter-height bounds are still outstanding.

ConicArithmetic:
- Proves the general completion-of-squares and norm identities.
- Proves the parabolic parameter reconstruction when alpha and J are nonzero,
  and injectivity of the nonparabolic norm-coordinate map.
- These are algebraic identities; the reduction of every actual component to
  this form, including the geometric nonvanishing hypotheses, is not yet done.

ConicNonparabolic:
- Proves a uniform count for every finite set of integer points on a conic
  with nonzero discriminant and nonzero norm, in terms of the explicit
  transformed-coordinate bound B and the norm integer N. It reuses the proved
  definite/indefinite norm bounds with tau(|N|)^3, which suffices for epsilon.
  Also proves the explicit bounds B = 11 K^2 H and |N| <= 116 K^4 from
  original coefficient height K and coordinate height H. The candidate's
  stronger tau(|N|) estimate is not needed or claimed here.

ConicRawDeterminant and ConicParabola:
- Generalize the existing muH4 polynomial to commutative rings and prove its
  exact six-factor Vandermonde leading term, with a remainder of gap degree <=5.
  Under quadratic gap substitution the remainder has parameter degree <=10,
  and a nonzero Vandermonde coefficient forces exact degree 12.
- Reuse the polynomial already occurring in the proved multiplicity identity;
  a separate new raw-Hankel identification is unnecessary for this argument.
- Prove that a rational function satisfying a monic polynomial equation over
  K[t] is a polynomial. Prove the quartic/remainder degree calculations:
  a degree-12 normalization with quadratic nodes forces deg(q)=3;
  the recurrence coefficients V and W have degrees 12 and 9 respectively.
- Prove the exact four-step remainder expansion and degree transfer through
  the scalar normalization identity.
- Proves the reduced denominator is nonconstant after cancellation: its
  degree exceeds the numerator degree by three.
- Geometric construction, exceptional cuts, height estimates and connection
  to the integer-value count remain to be assembled.

ConicWeights:
- Proves Jensen's cube-root weight transfer from the existing reciprocal sum.
- Proves the ninth-power minor-pattern weight bound
    (sum_p shortR(p)^(-1/3))^9 <= C X^44 tau^12.
  This retains sufficient divisor slack, including nonprimitive patterns.
  For X bounded by a fixed power of a, the divisor loss is subpolynomial.

ConicExponent, ConicWindow, ConicGlobalReduction and ConicLocalEstimate:
- Prove all three abstract exponent implications and their versions in the
  repository's actual remainder-window coordinates.
- Prove the complete integer-rounding and line-scale dichotomy, including the

Shared geometry
---------------
The FixedDividend development now supplies actual orbit identities, moment
positivity, absolutely irreducible quadratic equations, coprimality, a finite
height-two prime decomposition, at most 24 complex components, and eventual
Hilbert degrees between 1 and 24. CriticalGrowth and DegreeOne identify
slope-one components as lines; ComponentLineExclusion excludes them through
relevant actual orbit points. HyperplaneDegree proves that any proper
hyperplane section has at most d points, and hence proves planarity for
degree-two components with at least three distinct points.
RationalComponents, RationalCurves and RationalOrbit now also supply these
interfaces directly over Q: all rational zeros are covered by at most 24
rational prime components of degree in [1,24], and every component through an
actual large-scale orbit point has degree in [2,24]. Rational components without
rational points are discarded. No unproved complex-to-rational degree descent
hypothesis is used. The degree-two planarity theorem works over Q as well.
See pierce_18_61_status.txt for the earlier component-by-component record.
