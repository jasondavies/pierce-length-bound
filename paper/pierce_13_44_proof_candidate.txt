# A fixed-dividend refinement for the Pierce remainder iteration

**Status:** research proof candidate, derived and checked in this conversation on 19 October 2026. It has not received external mathematical review or formal verification. No priority claim is made. The only non-elementary counting input is the coefficient-uniform integral-point bound for affine curves stated in Section 9. The original logarithmic-squared conjecture is not proved here.

## Claim

For every epsilon > 0,

\[
P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{13/44+\varepsilon}
\]

uniformly for integers a > b > 0, where P is the number of positive states in b_(i+1) = a mod b_i.

The new local estimate is

\[
T\ll X^5 H^{1/2}\log(2H)+X^4H\log(2X),
\qquad H\asymp Q/T,\quad X\asymp aH^6/Q^4.
\]

Unlike the preceding 45/152 argument, this proof does not use a weighted count of lattices or a Pell-equation estimate. It does use an external uniform bound for points on curves.

## 1. Positive functionals and the support convention

Write a = q_i b_i + b_(i+1), with b_0,...,b_(N-1) positive and b_N = 0. The quotients strictly increase: a = q_i b_i + b_(i+1) > (q_i+1)b_(i+1). Put k_i=q_(i+1)-q_i >=1.

All local identities in this note are applied only to starts having sixteen subsequent positive states and sixteen subsequent quotient gaps within the selected quotient block. This deliberately exceeds the support requirements. In particular, a terminal point mass must not be assigned the nonterminal first moment 1.

Define finite positive probability measures backwards. At the terminal state set mu_(N-1)=delta_0. Given masses w_x of mu_(i+1), place mass w_x/(k_i+x) at k_i+x and the remaining mass at zero. The masses are positive. The last gap is at least two: if a=q_last*b_last=q_prev*b_prev+b_last, then (q_last-1)b_last=q_prev*b_prev>q_prev*b_last. At earlier steps positivity follows either from k_i>=2, or from the fact that the next measure has positive mass away from zero.

These measures satisfy

\[
\mu_i(xf(x))=\mu_{i+1}(f(k_i+x)).
\]

Set d nu_i(x)=d mu_i(x)/(q_i+x). Then

\[
\nu_i(1)=b_i/a,\qquad (q_i+x)\nu_i=\mu_i.
\]

Indeed q_i nu_i(1)+nu_(i+1)(1)=1, with the correct terminal value. Multiplication of nu_i by x, followed by translation by -k_i, gives nu_(i+1).

At a fixed start use local indexing, q=q_i, s_j=q_(i+j)-q_i, and

\[
F_j(x)=\prod_{v=0}^{j-1}(x-s_v),\qquad F_0=1.
\]

Then

\[
\mu_i(F_jf)=\mu_{i+j}(f(s_j+x)),\qquad
\nu_i(F_j)=b_{i+j}/a.
\]

In particular mu_i(F_j)=1. With the stated support margin,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_i(xF_j)&=s_j+1,\\
\mu_i(x^2F_j)&=s_j^2+2s_j+1+k_j,\\
\mu_i(x^3F_j)&=s_j^3+3s_j^2+3s_j(1+k_j)+(1+k_j)^2+k_{j+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

All raw moments of mu_i are integers, by backward induction. If v_m=nu_i(x^m), then

\[
v_m=(-q_i)^m b_i/a+z_m,\qquad z_m\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Consequently, a times every square minor of the weighted moment matrix is integral: its nonintegral part has rank one. Define

\[
H_r(i)=\det[\mu_i(x^{u+v})]_{u,v<r},\qquad
I_r(i)=a\det[\nu_i(x^{u+v})]_{u,v<r}.
\]

These are positive when sufficiently many support points remain, and I_r(i) is an integer.

## 2. The minor pattern and its uniform height

Let

\[
d_j=b_{i+j}-b_{i+j+1},\qquad
r_j=k_j(s_j+s_{j+1})+k_{j+1}.
\]

Form the integer 3 by 5 matrix with rows (d_j), (k_j), (r_j), for j=0,...,4. Denote its maximal minors by p_(j,l,m). Set

\[
A=p_{012},\quad A'=p_{123},\quad A''=p_{234},\quad
U=p_{023},\quad V=-p_{013},\quad U'=p_{134},\quad V'=-p_{124}.
\]

The first three labels are I_4(i), I_4(i+1), I_4(i+2), hence positive. For example, change the rows of the rank-four Gram determinant to 1, q+x, x(q+x), x^2(q+x), then take adjacent column differences. The resulting 3 by 3 determinant is p_012/a before multiplication by a. Shifting gives the other two labels.

The cofactor kernel vectors of the first and last four columns give

\[
C=(-\rho,u,v,1),\qquad C^+=(-\rho',u',v',1),
\]

where

\[
\rho=A'/A,\ u=U/A,\ v=V/A,\qquad
\rho'=A''/A',\ u'=U'/A',\ v'=V'/A'.
\]

They annihilate each of the three matrix rows on their respective four-column supports.

For q in [Q,2Q) and the needed gaps at most H, every maximal minor satisfies

\[
|p_{jlm}|\ll aH^6/Q^4.
\]

To prove this without a scale hypothesis, use the exact expansion

\[
\frac1{q+y}=\frac1q-\frac y{q^2}+\frac{y^2}{q^3}-\frac{y^3}{q^3(q+y)}.
\]

Positivity and the explicit third moments give, uniformly for the finitely many j used,

\[
\frac{b_{i+j}}a=\frac1q-\frac{s_j+1}{q^2}
+\frac{s_j^2+2s_j+1+k_j}{q^3}+O(H^3/q^4).
\]

Subtract adjacent expressions:

\[
\frac{d_j}a=\frac{k_j}{q^2}-\frac{r_j+k_j}{q^3}+O(H^3/q^4).
\]

Subtract a(q^(-2)-q^(-3)) times the k-row and add a q^(-3) times the r-row to the d-row. The three resulting row sizes are O(aH^3/q^4), O(H), O(H^2), proving the minor bound.

Choose X=C aH^6/Q^4 for a sufficiently large absolute C. If a selected start exists, A>=1 ensures X>=1. All ten minors are then bounded by X.

The full minor vector determines the seven displayed coordinates. Conversely those seven determine the remaining three through

\[
A'p_{014}=VV'-AU',\quad A'p_{024}=-AA''-UV',\quad
A'p_{034}=UU'+VA''.
\]

## 3. Two elementary counts for minor patterns

### 3.1 All patterns: O(X^5)

Choose a maximal minor of largest absolute value m. After one of finitely many permutations and a sign choice, use it as a pivot block B. The other columns give integral vectors u=adj(B)w_4 and v=adj(B)w_5, with coordinates bounded by m. Remaining minors are signed coordinates of (u cross v)/m. Hence u cross v=0 modulo m.

For fixed u and g=gcd(m,u_1,u_2,u_3), the number of v modulo m satisfying this condition is m g^2. To see this write m=gn,u=g u_0. Since gcd(n,u_0)=1, u_0 cross v=0 modulo n means v is a scalar multiple of u_0. There are n such residue vectors modulo n and g^3 lifts each modulo m.

Allowing the bounded number of representatives in [-m,m], the count at pivot m is at most

\[
Cm\sum_{|u_j|\le m}\gcd(m,u_1,u_2,u_3)^2
\ll m^4\sum_{g\mid m}g^{-1}.
\]

Summing over m<=X gives O(X^5), because after writing m=gr the resulting sum is bounded by X^5 sum_g g^(-2). This counts nonprimitive vectors as well.

### 3.2 A specified minor vanishes: O(X^4 log(2X))

By a fixed integral coordinate change and exterior duality, it suffices to count decomposable integral bivectors w in Z^5 of height O(X) with w_45=0. Write

\[
a=(w_{14},w_{24},w_{34}),\quad b=(w_{15},w_{25},w_{35}),\quad
t=(w_{23},-w_{13},w_{12}).
\]

The Pluecker relations give a cross b=0 and t dot a=t dot b=0. If a=b=0, there are O(X^3) possibilities. Otherwise write

\[
a=r z,\qquad b=s z
\]

with primitive nonzero z in Z^3 and integers r,s not both zero. Put Z=||z||_infinity. Then Z<=C X, |r|,|s|<<X/Z, and z dot t=0.

For primitive z with maximal coordinate of size Z, there are O(X^2/Z) vectors t of height O(X) satisfying z dot t=0. Indeed, choose the maximal coordinate as the third one. The other two coordinates satisfy one surjective congruence modulo Z, which has Z residue pairs; counting representatives in the X-box proves the estimate.

In a dyadic range of Z there are O(Z^3) choices of z. Thus that range contributes

\[
O(Z^3)\,O((X/Z)^2)\,O(X^2/Z)=O(X^4).
\]

Summing dyadically proves O(X^4 log(2X)). The argument includes all scalar contents of w.

## 4. The two quartics and the first quadratic

Define

\[
P(x)=\sum_{m=0}^3C_m(F_{m+1}(x)-F_m(x))
=\sum_{j=0}^4c_jF_j(x).
\]

Then

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)
=(\rho,-\rho-u,u-v,v-1,1).
\]

Define c'_j similarly from C^+. The shifted next quartic is

\[
R(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c'_j\prod_{v=1}^{j}(x-s_v),
\quad
the product for j=0 being 1.
\]

Thus

\[
xR(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c'_jF_{j+1}(x).
\]

The row kernel equations imply that P is the monic degree-four orthogonal polynomial for nu_i; R is the next state's polynomial after translation. One can verify this directly: nu_i(P)=0 comes from the d-row, mu_i(P)=0 from sum c_j=0, and mu_i(xP)=mu_i(x^2P)=0 from the k- and r-rows. The relation mu_i=(q+x)nu_i gives orthogonality against all degrees <=3.

The signs rho>0, u>0, v<0 hold exactly. For the latter two put

\[
S(a,b,c)=a^2b+(a-1)b^2+ac,
\quad E(a,b,c,d)=a^2c+(2a-1)bc+ac^2+ad.
\]

The Cramer denominator is D_2=S(k_1,k_2,k_3)>0, and

\[
D_2u=\rho E(k_0,k_1,k_2,k_3)+S(k_2,k_3,k_4)>0,
\]
\[
-D_2v=\rho S(k_0,k_1,k_2)+E(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4)>0.
\]

In particular all c_j are nonzero, c_0,c_2,c_4>0, c_1<0, and c_3<-1.

Use x_1=k_1, x_2=k_2, x_3=k_3 as independent gap coordinates. The two linear relations give

\[
k_0=(u x_1+v x_2+x_3)/\rho,\qquad
k_4=\rho'x_1-u'x_2-v'x_3.
\]

The first quadratic is

\[
f=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j(s_j^2+k_j)=0.
\]

Equivalently it is sum_(m=0)^3 C_m r_m=0. Its homogeneous part is

\[
f_2=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^2,
\quad s_0=0,\quad s_4=-c_1s_1-c_2s_2-c_3s_3.
\]

The change between (x_1,x_2,x_3) and (s_1,s_2,s_3) is invertible. In the latter coordinates the determinant of the homogeneous quadratic matrix is -c_0c_1c_2c_3, which is nonzero. Therefore f is absolutely irreducible.

The next quadratic determines k_5:

\[
k_5=\frac{A''r_1-U'r_2-V'r_3}{A'}-k_4(s_4+s_5).
\]

For the polynomial argument we substitute this rational quadratic expression without imposing integrality or its interval bound. This only enlarges the set counted.

## 5. The fixed-dividend identity

Define the polynomial in the three independent gap coordinates

\[
D=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j\{s_j^3+3s_j^2+3s_j(1+k_j)+(1+k_j)^2+k_{j+1}\}.
\]

It has degree at most three and homogeneous cubic part

\[
D_3=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^3.
\]

At every actual start,

\[
D=\mu_i(x^3P)=\nu_i(P^2)>0.
\]

If p_5 is the monic quintic orthogonal polynomial for nu_i, then xR=p_5+gamma P for some scalar gamma, since both monic degree-five polynomials are orthogonal to degrees <=3. Thus Res(P,xR)=Res(P,p_5).

Writing tau_r=det[nu_i(x^(j+k))]_(j,k<r), the standard three-term orthogonal recurrence gives

\[
\operatorname{Res}(P,p_5)=\tau_5^4/\tau_4^5.
\]

This follows by applying the recurrence successively at roots of the preceding polynomial: the resultant is the product of beta_j^j for j=1,...,4, where beta_j=tau_(j+1)tau_(j-1)/tau_j^2. Its sign for rank four is positive. Since tau_4=A/a and tau_5/tau_4=D,

\[
\boxed{A\operatorname{Res}(P,xR)=aD^4.}
\]

Put Omega=A Res(P,xR)-aD^4. After clearing denominators this is a polynomial of degree at most twelve in the three gap variables, and every actual start lies on f=Omega=0.

### Degree and leading form of the resultant

For fixed coefficient patterns,

\[
\deg\operatorname{Res}(P,xR)=10,
\]

and its degree-ten part is

\[
c_0c_1c_2c_3\prod_{0\le j<l\le4}(s_l-s_j).
\]

Here the product is not identically zero: the pattern occurs at strictly increasing nodes. To prove the formula, scale any strictly increasing node shape by t. The root of P near t s_j has displacement O(t^(j-3)), j=0,...,3. At that root, use P=0 to eliminate F_4 in F_5=(x-t s_4)F_4. The leading value of xR is

\[
c_j(s_4-s_j)\prod_{v<j}(s_j-s_v)t^{j+1}.
\]

Lower Newton terms have degree at most j there. Multiplying over the roots proves the formula, first on an open set of node shapes and then as a polynomial identity.

### The two equations have no common surface component

The quadratic f_2 does not divide D_3. In the independent coordinates (s_1,s_2,s_3), write b=c_2 and c=c_3. If D_3=f_2 L with L linear, comparison of the s_2^3 and s_3^3 coefficients forces L's corresponding coefficients to be 1-b and 1-c. (Here b>0 and c<-1.) The coefficient of s_2^2 s_3 then forces 1+b+c=0. But 1+c_2+c_3=u>0. This is impossible.

Since f is irreducible, if it divided Omega then f_2 would divide the degree-twelve leading term -aD_3^4 and hence D_3, a contradiction. Therefore f and Omega are coprime. Their common zeros have total curve degree at most 24, by Bezout. Possible zero-dimensional pieces have bounded size and can also be included in the counts below.

## 6. Lines force a simple relation between the minors

Suppose an affine line is contained in f=Omega=0. Along that line D has degree at most three and the resultant has degree at most ten. The identity A Res=aD^4 forces deg D<=2. The resultant then has degree at most eight.

Let z_j be the leading node coordinates of the line, with z_0=0. The line direction is nonzero. Its leading equations give

\[
\sum_{j=0}^4c_jz_j^m=0\quad(m=0,1,2,3),
\qquad
\prod_{j<l}(z_l-z_j)=0.
\]

Group equal z_j. Since there are at most four distinct cluster values, a Vandermonde system forces the sum of the c_j in each cluster to vanish. No singleton cluster is possible, since all c_j are nonzero. A single cluster would force the line direction to be zero. Therefore the five nodes split into clusters of sizes two and three; some pair c_j+c_l vanishes.

The signs permit only four possibilities:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{pair}&\text{minor relation}\\
\hline
c_0+c_3=0&A'+V-A=0\\
c_1+c_2=0&A'+V=0\\
c_2+c_3=0&U-A=0\\
c_1+c_4=0&A-A'-U=0.
\end{array}
\]

Each relation is a single vanishing minor after a bounded unimodular column change. If v_0,...,v_4 are the columns of M, the following determinants express them, up to a harmless sign:

\[
\begin{aligned}
A'+V-A&=\det(v_1,v_2+v_0,v_3-v_0),\\
A'+V&=\det(v_1,v_2+v_0,v_3),\\
U-A&=\det(v_0,v_2,v_3+v_1),\\
A'+U-A&=\det(v_0+v_1,v_2,v_3+v_1).
\end{aligned}
\]

The transformations and their inverses have bounded integer entries and determinant +/-1. Hence Section 3.2 bounds all line-supporting patterns by O(X^4 log(2X)). This count is only a necessary-condition bound; it does not assume that every exceptional pattern actually supports a line.

## 7. Bounded multiplicity of states

A fixed complete minor pattern and a fixed integer triple (k_1,k_2,k_3) determine k_0,k_4 and P. They also determine H_4, since the raw moments through degree six depend only on k_0,...,k_4 with the stated support margin.

The orthogonal-polynomial determinant formula gives

\[
A P(-q_i)=aH_4.
\]

For a direct verification, set x=-q_i in the 5 by 5 monic-quartic determinant and, from right to left, add q_i times each preceding column to the next column. The last row becomes (1,0,0,0,0), and the remaining determinant consists of the raw mu moments.

The displayed equation is a nonzero degree-four equation in q_i. Consequently there are at most four possible integer quotients for the fixed data. Quotients strictly increase along an orbit, so at most four starts have those data. No separate reconstruction of b_i is needed.

## 8. Local count

All nonlinear irreducible curve components have degree between two and 24. The external theorem stated below bounds their integral points in the central gap box [1,H]^3 by O(H^(1/2) log(2H)), per complete pattern, uniformly in all coefficients and in a.

There are O(X^5) total patterns. Line components can occur only in O(X^4 log(2X)) patterns, by Section 6. Each such pattern has at most 24 line components, and a line has O(H) integer points in the H-box (project to any nonconstant coordinate). Multiplicity at most four changes only the absolute constant.

Thus

\[
\boxed{T\ll X^5H^{1/2}\log(2H)+X^4H\log(2X).}
\]

No congruence-density assumption, nonvanishing modulo primes, Pell estimate, or weighted lattice count is used in this step.

## 9. External counting input

For an affine irreducible curve C over Q of degree d in A^n, n>=2, and B>=1,

\[
\#\{x\in C(\mathbb Z):|x_j|\le B\}
\ll_n d^3 B^{1/d}(\log B+d).
\]

The implied constant is independent of the coefficients. This is Corollary 2.2 of:

R. Cluckers, P. Debes, Y. I. Hendel, K. H. Nguyen, F. Vermeulen, *Improvements on dimension growth results and effective Hilbert's irreducibility theorem*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 13 (2025), e153, DOI 10.1017/fms.2025.10096. That corollary attributes the curve estimate to Castryck, Cluckers, Dittmann and Nguyen, *The dimension growth conjecture, polynomial in the degree and without logarithmic factors*, Algebra & Number Theory 14 (2020).

Primary full-text source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/improvements-on-dimension-growth-results-and-effective-hilberts-irreducibility-theorem/A7BCAEBF00B8B165D6DDB3FFB9B40B97

Irreducibility over Q is sufficient in this statement. This matters when decomposing f=Omega=0 over Q. All degrees in the present application are bounded by 24.

## 10. Global exponent

In a quotient block [Q,2Q), the internal gaps sum to less than Q. Choose H=C_0 Q/T with a sufficiently large absolute C_0. Discard the final sixteen starts and starts whose next sixteen gaps contain one larger than H. An oversized gap spoils at most sixteen starts, so a fixed positive proportion remains when T is large. The bounded T cases are harmless.

The drop estimate is, for every eta>0,

\[
Q\ll_\eta a^{1+\eta}/T^2.
\]

Indeed drops in the block, including its final drop out, sum to at most a/Q. At least half are <=2a/(QT), and a+d_i=(q_i+1)b_i. For each drop value there are at most tau(a+d_i) possible distinct remainders. The elementary estimate tau(n)<<_eta n^eta proves the displayed inequality. If the drop interval contains no positive integer, the supposed block cannot exist.

Absorb logarithms in a^eta, noting H<<a and X<<a^7. Substitute

\[
H\asymp Q/T,\qquad X\asymp aQ^2/T^6.
\]

If the nonlinear-component term dominates, then

\[
T\ll_\eta a^{5+\eta}Q^{21/2}/T^{61/2},
\]

so

\[
T^{63}\ll_\eta a^{10+2\eta}Q^{21},
\qquad
T^{105}\ll_\eta a^{31+23\eta}.
\]

If the line term dominates, then

\[
T\ll_\eta a^{4+\eta}Q^9/T^{25},
\]

so

\[
T^{26}\ll_\eta a^{4+\eta}Q^9,
\qquad
T^{44}\ll_\eta a^{13+10\eta}.
\]

Since 31/105 < 13/44, every block has T<<_epsilon a^(13/44+epsilon). Choose eta sufficiently small before summing the O(log a) quotient blocks. The finite range of small a is absorbed into the constant. This proves the claimed candidate bound.

The saving relative to 45/152 is

\[
45/152-13/44=1/1672.
\]

The line-pattern contribution is now the bottleneck. The proof does not establish the smaller conditional exponent 18/61 mentioned in the preceding conversation.

## 11. What happened to the sixth-gap congruence?

It can be weaker than its displayed modulus suggests. Let

\[
g=\gcd\{p_{jlm}\},\qquad h=\gcd(A,A',U,V).
\]

Let L be the primitive integer lattice in the row space of M, and project to the first four coordinates. Projection is injective on that row space because A'>0. If W is the saturation of the projected lattice, then

\[
[W:\pi L]=h/g.
\]

To prove this, choose a primitive lattice basis of L. Its minors are the primitive vector p/g. The gcd of the projected maximal minors is h/g, which is precisely the index in the saturation, as follows by integer diagonal reduction of a lattice basis.

The first quadratic says that (r_0,...,r_3) lies in W. The sixth-gap congruence says it belongs to the smaller image pi L. Thus, when h=g, the congruence is automatic on the first quadratic, even if A' is large.

For example, at a=23399, b=14735, the labels are

(13974,5203,8500,24053,-74746,81926,-20958),

and h=g=1. The apparent modulus 5203 supplies no extra congruence restriction after the lattice and first quadratic have been imposed.

The fixed-dividend resultant avoids this redundancy by using a itself as an exact normalization, rather than assuming a modular density saving.

## 12. An explicit family removed by the normalization

At a=473, b=299, the labels are

(108,24,12,156,-240,48,-60).

The two linear relations, first quadratic, and the integrality/interval condition on the sixth gap all admit

\[
k_0=k_1=\cdots=k_5=t
\]

for every positive integer t. On this line, the fixed-dividend polynomial is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega(t)={}&-\frac{16}{81}t^6(t-1)\bigl(1135673t^5+1135673t^4\\
&\hspace{30mm}+446633t^3+97859t^2+13518t+1080\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

All coefficients of the last factor are positive. Hence only t=1 survives for positive t. This is a concrete check that the new equality can eliminate a family untouched by the congruence and interval restriction.

## 13. Verification record

A fresh finite-measure implementation checked 200 actual starts, each with at least ten positive states. It verified positivity of the finite measures, orthogonality of both quartics, the raw and weighted Gram identities, D=nu(P^2), A P(-q)=aH_4, and A Res(P,xR)=aD^4, all in exact rational arithmetic.

Separate symbolic tests verified the resultant's degree-ten leading coefficient in seven examples, the coefficient comparison proving f_2 does not divide D_3, and 200 determinant equalities implementing the four bounded column changes.

Exploratory tests with shorter windows are not used to validate the finite moment argument: near termination, displayed formulas involving nonterminal moments require support padding. The proof above always retains sixteen future gaps.

These computations are algebraic checks, not a replacement for the uniform proof. Neither external review nor Lean verification has been completed.
