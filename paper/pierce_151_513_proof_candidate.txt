# An integral-coordinate lift for the Pierce remainder iteration

## Status and claim

Research proof candidate, 19 October 2026. This note extends the uploaded
`pierce_180_611_proof_candidate.md` and its finite-moment and line-exclusion
foundations in `pierce_18_61_proof_candidate.md`. It has not received external
mathematical review or formal verification. No priority claim is made.

The new ingredient is to add the smaller integral Hankel invariant I_3 as
an affine curve coordinate. A degree-d nonexceptional curve carrying
sufficiently many actual states then has a graph of degree at least 5d/2.
At actual states I_3 is small enough that this degree increase is useful.

Subject to the retained foundational statements identified below, the
resulting bound is

\[
\boxed{P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{151/513+\varepsilon}},
\qquad 151/513=0.29434697855750486\ldots.
\]

The saving over the preceding candidate is

\[
180/611-151/513=79/313443.
\]

This argument does **not** use the weighted-lattice incidence estimate.
It also avoids the arithmetic classification of higher-genus curves. The
only external uniform arithmetic point-counting input remains the affine
curve theorem in Section 8. The geometric divisor facts used in Section 6
are stated explicitly and referenced at the end.

The original logarithmic-squared conjecture is not resolved.

## 1. Retained framework and notation

For integers a>b>0, write

\[
a=q_i b_i+b_{i+1},\qquad k_i=q_{i+1}-q_i>0
\]

until the first zero remainder. Positive remainders decrease strictly and
quotients increase strictly. A quotient block is Q <= q_i < 2Q; let T count
its states. For sufficiently large T, a fixed positive fraction of its starts
have their next sixteen gaps bounded by H=C_0 Q/T. The last sixteen starts
are discarded. This is the support margin throughout this note.

Put

\[
X=C_2 aH^6/Q^4,\qquad K=Q^4/(aH^5),\qquad
S=C_3 aH^3/Q^3,
\tag{1.1}
\]

where the positive absolute constants are sufficiently large for the stated
bounds. A represented pattern has X>=1; a selected actual state also has S>=1.

We retain the following inputs, without treating numerical tests as proofs
of them:

1. There are finite positive functionals mu_i and nu_i with
   \[
   (q_i+x)\nu_i=\mu_i,\quad \mu_i(1)=\mu_i(x)=1,\quad
   \nu_i(1)=b_i/a.
   \]
   For s_j=q_(i+j)-q_i and F_j(x)=product_(v<j)(x-s_v),
   \[
   \mu_i(F_j f)=\mu_{i+j}(f(s_j+x)),\quad \mu_i(F_j)=1.
   \]
   Their raw moments m_j=mu_i(x^j) through degree six are polynomials in
   the first five gaps; for j>=1 their gap degrees are at most j-1.
   The finite support convention is essential here.

2. The quantities
   \[
   H_r(i)=\det[m_{u+v}]_{u,v<r},\qquad
   I_r(i)=a\det[\nu_i(x^{u+v})]_{u,v<r}
   \]
   are positive for the ranks used here, and the I_r(i) are integers.
   Integrality follows because the nonintegral part of the weighted moment
   matrix has rank one. Positive Gram comparison gives
   \[
   0<I_r(i)\le a H_r(i)/q_i^r.
   \tag{1.2}
   \]

3. Form the 3-by-5 integer matrix with rows
   \[
   d_j=b_{i+j}-b_{i+j+1},\quad k_j,\quad
   r_j=k_j(s_j+s_{j+1})+k_{j+1},\quad 0\le j\le4.
   \]
   All ten maximal minors p have height at most X. There are O(X^5)
   possible full minor vectors, including every common factor. The proof
   uses a maximal pivot m and the congruence u cross v=0 mod m; for fixed
   u and g=gcd(m,u_1,u_2,u_3), there are exactly m g^2 admissible residue
   vectors v. Summing gives O(X^5).

4. Set
   \[
   A=p_{012},\ A'=p_{123},\ A''=p_{234},\quad
   U=p_{023},\ V=-p_{013},\ U'=p_{134},\ V'=-p_{124}.
   \]
   Then A,A',A''>0. Put rho=A'/A, u=U/A, v=V/A and define rho',u',v'
   using the next labels. Exactly rho>0,u>0,v<0. The Newton coefficient
   vectors of the two quartics are
   \[
   c=(\rho,-\rho-u,u-v,v-1,1),
   \]
   and c' defined similarly. With
   \[
   P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c_jF_j(x),\qquad
   Z(x)=xR(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c'_jF_{j+1}(x),
   \]
   P and Z annihilate nu_i against all polynomials of degree <=3.

5. For a fixed pattern, the two linear relations recover k_0,k_4 from
   y=(k_1,k_2,k_3):
   \[
   k_0=(u k_1+v k_2+k_3)/\rho,\qquad
   k_4=\rho'k_1-u'k_2-v'k_3.
   \]
   Thus s_0,...,s_5 are homogeneous linear functions of y. The first
   quadratic f=0 and the fixed-dividend equation
   \[
   A\operatorname{Res}(P,Z)=aD^4
   \]
   cut out curves in A^3 of total degree at most 24; they have no common
   surface component. D is the polynomial quartic norm, after the next
   quadratic has eliminated k_5. Actual states have D>0.

6. A pattern is exceptional if some c_j+c_l=0, j!=l. Exactly four such
   relations are possible:
   \[
   A'+V-A=0,\quad A'+V=0,\quad U-A=0,\quad A-A'-U=0.
   \]
   Each is one vanishing maximal minor after a bounded unimodular column
   change. The number of exceptional full patterns is O(X^4 log(2X)).
   A proof reminder is given in Section 9.

7. At every branch at infinity of a nonexceptional component, the five node
   leading values z_0,...,z_4 are pairwise distinct, with z_0=0. If e is
   the pole order of a generic gap coordinate at that branch, then e is
   positive and even. The infinity divisor has total degree d for a
   degree-d component. In particular nonexceptional geometric components
   have even degree. These are the parity and noncollision results in
   Section 3 of the 180/611 candidate.

8. If K>=C_1 X, no selected actual state lies on a line component. In the
   complementary range K<C_1 X, the drop estimate yields exponent 5/17.
   This is the retained line-exclusion lemma, not a consequence of a
   numerical search.

The raw determinant expansions needed below are

\[
H_r=\prod_{0\le j<l<r}(s_l-s_j)
       +O_{\rm degree}\bigl(r(r-1)/2-1\bigr),
\tag{1.3}
\]

where the notation means a polynomial of no greater gap degree. They follow
by using the monic Newton basis in the Gram determinant: a mixed moment
mu(F_i F_j) has gap degree at most min(i,j), and only the diagonal
permutation reaches the leading Vandermonde degree.

These retained inputs concern exact identities and uniform estimates. The
new results begin with the reconstruction and determinant calculation below.

## 2. Reconstruct a rational weighted functional

For fixed P of degree four and Z of degree five, with nonzero resultant,
the eight polynomials

\[
P,xP,x^2P,x^3P,\quad Z,xZ,x^2Z,x^3Z
\]

are linearly independent. A dependence gives U P+V Z=0 with deg U,deg V<=3;
coprimality forces P to divide V, so V=U=0. Hence their annihilator in the
nine-dimensional space of functionals on degrees <=8 is one-dimensional.

Let h_0,...,h_8 be the signed maximal-minor kernel vector of the 8-by-9
coefficient matrix. Define rational functions

\[
q=\frac{h_1-h_2}{h_1-h_0},\qquad
v_j=\frac{h_j(h_1-h_0)}{h_1^2-h_0h_2}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

At an actual state these equal q_i and nu_i(x^j). To verify this, normalize
h_j/h_0=nu_i(x^j)/nu_i(1). The two conditions mu_i(1)=mu_i(x)=1 determine
q and the scale of nu exactly as in (2.1).

All displayed denominators are nonzero at actual states. Positive
definiteness gives

\[
\nu_i(x^2)\nu_i(1)-\nu_i(x)^2>0.
\]

If h_1=h_0, the two normalization equations would force equality in this
inequality. Also h_0!=0 because the actual annihilator has positive mass,
and the coefficient matrix has rank eight.

Define the reconstructed integer-coordinate function

\[
\mathcal I(y)=a\det[v_{r+s}]_{r,s=0}^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At actual states, this is the positive integer I_3(i).

### Bounded-degree cuts, not silent identities

On a fixed irreducible curve component C, impose the following additional
actual-state equations:

\[
qv_j+v_{j+1}=m_j(y),\qquad 0\le j\le6,
\tag{2.3}
\]

where m_j are the raw moment polynomials from the first five gaps, and

\[
A P(-q)=aH_4(y).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Their denominators do not vanish at actual states. Their degrees are
bounded absolutely, independently of a and of the pattern.

If any equation is not an identity in the function field of C, clearing
its denominator gives a nonzero polynomial on C of bounded degree.
Bezout bounds its zeros by an absolute constant. Such a component
contributes only O(1) actual gap points and hence O(1) actual states.

Otherwise all of (2.3)--(2.4) hold identically. The remainder of the argument
applies to this case. Components where the reconstruction denominators
vanish identically, or where Res(P,Z) vanishes identically, have no actual
states: the corresponding quantities are nonzero at every actual state.

This explicit cut-or-identity step is important. The original algebraic
curve is not assumed to consist entirely of orbit data.

### Uniform degree bound for the graph

The coefficients of P,Z have bounded degrees in y (a bound of five
suffices). Thus the h_j have degree at most 40. Formula (2.2) can be written
as

\[
\mathcal I
=a\frac{(h_1-h_0)^3\det[h_{r+s}]_{r,s<3}}
        {(h_1^2-h_0h_2)^3}.
\]

Its numerator and denominator have degrees at most 240. The graph of
mathcal I over a degree-d curve therefore has degree at most 241d: a generic
hyperplane of the graph pulls back, after clearing the denominator, to a
polynomial of degree at most 241 in the original coordinates. In particular
all graph degrees below are bounded by 24*241. This large absolute constant
is harmless for coefficient-uniform curve counting.

## 3. A resolvent identity for the smaller Hankel determinant

Write

\[
P(x)=x^4+p_3x^3+p_2x^2+p_1x+p_0,
\]

and define

\[
H_3=\det[m_{r+s}]_{r,s=0}^2,
\qquad
J=\det[m_{r+s}]_{r=0,1,2;\ s=0,1,3}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1

Where (2.3)--(2.4) hold identically,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I
=a\frac{(q-p_3)H_3-J}{P(-q)}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

**Proof.** Let G_nu=[v_(i+j)]_(i,j<4) and G_mu=[m_(i+j)]_(i,j<4).
Let T_P be the matrix of multiplication by x in the quotient basis
1,x,x^2,x^3 modulo P:

\[
T_P=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&-p_0\\
1&0&0&-p_1\\
0&1&0&-p_2\\
0&0&1&-p_3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The orthogonality equations v(x^jP)=0 for 0<=j<=3 and (2.3) give

\[
G_\mu=G_\nu(qI+T_P).
\]

Also det(qI+T_P)=P(-q). Take the bottom-right cofactor of G_nu, using
G_nu^(-1)=(qI+T_P)G_mu^(-1). The last row of qI+T_P has only the
entries 1 and q-p_3 in columns 2 and 3. The two corresponding adjugate
entries of G_mu are -J and H_3. This proves

\[
\det[v_{r+s}]_{r,s<3}=\frac{(q-p_3)H_3-J}{P(-q)}.
\]

Multiply by a. The calculation is a rational-function identity, so deriving
it on the open set where the matrices are invertible suffices. In the
present application H_4 is nonzero and (2.4) makes P(-q) nonzero; the same
matrix identity then makes G_nu invertible. □

The following degree estimates will prevent cancellation at infinity:

\[
\deg H_3=3,\qquad \deg J\le4,\qquad \deg p_3\le1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

In fact

\[
H_3=k_0^2k_1+(k_0-1)k_1^2+k_0k_2,
\tag{3.4}
\]

whose leading form is k_0k_1(k_0+k_1).

For J, change the row basis to F_0,F_1,F_2 and its first two columns to
F_0,F_1. Modulo the first two columns,

\[
x^3=F_3+(s_0+s_1+s_2)F_2.
\]

Consequently

\[
J=\det[\mu(F_iF_j)]_{i=0,1,2;\ j=0,1,3}
  +(s_0+s_1+s_2)H_3.
\]

The mixed determinant has degree at most three, since its moment entries
have degree at most min(i,j). Thus J has degree at most four and its leading
form is

\[
(s_0+s_1+s_2)\prod_{0\le j<l<3}(s_l-s_j).
\]

Finally p_3=c_3-(s_0+s_1+s_2+s_3), proving its degree bound.

## 4. Exact orders at every old point at infinity

Work over an algebraic closure and normalize the projective closure of a
nonexceptional component C on which the additional identities hold. Let p
be a point of the normalization above the original hyperplane at infinity.
Let e>0 be its multiplicity in that hyperplane divisor.

By the retained noncollision theorem, every node difference s_j-s_l has
pole order exactly e. The nonzero node functions s_1,...,s_4 have pole
order e; s_0=0. Their leading coefficients z_j are all distinct.

The raw determinant leading forms therefore give

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(H_4)=-6e,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_p(H_3)=-3e.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Quotient pole order

Equation (2.4) says A P(-q)=aH_4. If q had pole order at most e, then P(-q)
would have pole order at most 4e, contradicting (4.1). Let n>e be the pole
order of q. The monic q^4 term strictly dominates all other terms of P(-q),
so its pole order is 4n. Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_p(q)=-3e/2.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular e is even. This also recovers the even-multiplicity conclusion
for components surviving the additional actual-state identities.

### The smaller integer invariant vanishes

In the numerator of (3.2), qH_3 has pole order 9e/2. By (3.3), both
p_3H_3 and J have pole order at most 4e. Therefore there is no cancellation
of its leading term. The denominator P(-q) has pole order 6e. Thus

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_p(\mathcal I)=3e/2.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is an exact zero order, not just an upper or lower estimate. In
particular mathcal I is nonzero and has no poles at any original infinity
point. The reasoning applies separately to each branch, including branches
above singular points of the original projective curve.

## 5. The integer coordinate is small at actual states

At an actual selected start, (1.2) and (3.4) give

\[
1\le I_3(i)\le \frac{aH_3(i)}{q_i^3}
\ll\frac{aH^3}{Q^3}=S/C_3.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For instance H_3<=3H^3 when the three needed gaps are at most H>=1.
This is the reason to use I_3 rather than the much larger remainder b as
the extra coordinate.

The actual points of the graph

\[
\Gamma_C=\overline{\{(k_1,k_2,k_3,\mathcal I):
                     (k_1,k_2,k_3)\in C\}}\subset\mathbb A^4
\tag{5.2}
\]

are integer points in a box of radius O(max(H,S)). The graph is defined
over Q whenever C is, and the projection to its first three coordinates
is birational to C.

## 6. The graph has degree at least five halves of the old degree

### Lemma 6.1 (degree increase)

Let C be a degree-d geometrically irreducible nonexceptional curve surviving
the additional identities of Section 2. Then

\[
\boxed{\deg\Gamma_C\ge\frac52 d.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

**Proof.** On the nonsingular projective model let

\[
E=\sum_{p\mid\infty}e_p[p]
\]

be the pole divisor of a generic original affine linear function. Its degree
is d. Equations (4.3) give zeros of mathcal I at these points of total
multiplicity 3d/2.

A nonzero rational function on a smooth projective curve has zero and pole
divisors of equal degree. Therefore

\[
\deg(\mathcal I)_\infty
=\deg(\mathcal I)_0\ge3d/2.
\]

All these poles are at points lying over the original affine part, because
mathcal I has a zero at every point over the original infinity hyperplane.
They are consequently disjoint from E.

A generic affine linear function in the four graph coordinates has pole
divisor exactly

\[
E+(\mathcal I)_\infty.
\]

At old infinity points the gap coordinates dominate, with a generic linear
combination having pole order e_p. At a finite pole of mathcal I, the old
coordinates are regular and the nonzero coefficient of mathcal I prevents
cancellation. There are no other poles.

Since the graph map is birational, the degree of this generic hyperplane
pullback is the degree of the graph. It is at least d+3d/2, proving (6.1). □

Normalization handles singularities, and the divisor-degree statement is
invariant under birational proper maps of curves. References for these
facts are listed in Section 12.

If C is irreducible over Q but not geometrically irreducible, apply the
argument to its geometric components and add degrees. The rational graph
is still irreducible over Q, and its degree is at least 5d/2. Alternatively,
rational points confined to intersections of distinct conjugate components
form a bounded contribution. Either treatment is sufficient here.

In particular, an original nonexceptional component of degree at least four
produces a graph of degree at least ten, with degree bounded above by the
absolute constant from Section 2.

## 7. Nonexceptional conics: a direct divisor argument using the same coordinate

The retained parity theorem implies that a nonexceptional geometric conic
has exactly one point at infinity, of multiplicity two. If the conic is
defined over Q, the unique point is rational. It is a parabolic conic in its
plane. Nonexceptional degree-two components cannot split into lines over
the algebraic closure, by the same parity theorem.

If it contains at most two actual gap points, their contribution is bounded.
Otherwise choose three actual integer points. No three points of an
irreducible conic are collinear, so they determine a plane with integer
coefficients of height O(H^3). Project onto two original integer gap
coordinates, restrict f to the plane, and clear denominators. Because f
contains no plane, the restriction is the conic equation. Its degree is two
and its integer coefficient height is bounded by a fixed power of a.

After a bounded unimodular change of these two coordinates, the equation is

\[
\alpha x^2+\beta xy+\gamma y^2+dx+ey+f_0=0,
\quad\alpha\ne0,\quad\beta^2-4\alpha\gamma=0.
\]

Put

\[
t=2\alpha x+\beta y+d,\quad
J_0=2\beta d-4\alpha e,\quad E_0=d^2-4\alpha f_0.
\]

Then t is an integer at every integer gap point, and

\[
t^2=J_0y+E_0,
\quad
y=(t^2-E_0)/J_0,
\quad
x=(t-\beta y-d)/(2\alpha).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Geometric irreducibility gives J_0!=0. This is a birational polynomial
parametrization, with its unique infinity point at t=infinity.

The rational function mathcal I(t) has, by (4.3) with e=2, a zero of order
three at infinity. It is therefore nonzero and proper, and its reduced
denominator is nonconstant. Its degrees are bounded absolutely; its rational
coefficients, after clearing and cancelling denominators, have heights
bounded by a fixed power of a. The latter follows from (2.1), the bounded
minor heights, the plane construction above, and fixed-degree polynomial
factor bounds (or the elementary root bound and Gauss lemma).

We use the following elementary fact. If F,G in Z[t] are coprime over Q,
G is nonconstant, and R=Res(F,G)!=0, then

\[
F(t)/G(t)\in\mathbb Z,\quad t\in\mathbb Z,\quad G(t)\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G(t)\mid R.
\]

Indeed the polynomial Bezout identity U F+V G=R gives the divisibility.
For each signed divisor n of R, the equation G(t)=n has at most deg G roots.
Thus the number of such t is at most 2 deg(G) tau(|R|).

Apply this to mathcal I(t). Its values are positive integers at actual
states. The resultant has size at most a fixed power of a, and the elementary
divisor estimate tau(n)<<_eta n^eta gives O_eta(a^eta) actual parameters.
Each parameter determines the gap point, and the reconstructed q determines
the state because quotients are strictly increasing. Hence

\[
\boxed{\text{nonexceptional conic contribution}\ll_\eta a^\eta X^5.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This proves the needed conic estimate with the new integer coordinate. It
does not rely on a norm-equation estimate or on the earlier properness
argument for the reconstructed remainder b.

## 8. Count the higher-degree graphs

The arithmetic input is the coefficient-uniform affine-curve theorem:
for an affine curve C over Q, irreducible over Q, of degree d in fixed
ambient dimension n>=2,

\[
\#\{x\in C(\mathbb Z):|x_j|\le B\}
\ll_n d^3 B^{1/d}(\log B+d).
\tag{8.1}
\]

It is Corollary 2.2 of Cluckers--Debes--Hendel--Nguyen--Vermeulen (2025),
attributed there to Castryck--Cluckers--Dittmann--Nguyen (2020). Its constant
is independent of coefficient height.

For each surviving nonexceptional component of degree at least four,
Section 6 gives graph degree at least ten and at most an absolute constant.
Section 5 bounds the actual integer coordinates by

\[
B\ll\max(H,S).
\]

Consequently each such component contributes at most

\[
C\max(H,S)^{1/10}\log(2\max(H,S)).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The number and total degrees of the original components are bounded
absolutely per pattern. The O(1) actual-point contributions from additional
cuts in Section 2 are included separately. For every actual gap point, the
reconstruction (2.1) gives the unique quotient; the retained multiplicity
bound of four would also suffice.

There are O(X^5) full patterns. Therefore the higher-degree contribution is

\[
\ll X^5\max(H,S)^{1/10}\log(2\max(H,S)).
\tag{8.3}
\]

No lattice change of variables and no count of short vectors is used.

## 9. Exceptional patterns and the full local bound

We retain the O(X^4 log(2X)) exceptional-pattern count. To recall the
arithmetic reason, after a bounded column change and exterior duality a
specified coordinate of an integral decomposable bivector is zero. Write
its two proportional three-coordinate blocks as r z and s z, with z
primitive of height Z, and let the remaining three-vector be t. Then

\[
|r|,|s|\ll X/Z,\qquad z\cdot t=0,\qquad |t_j|\ll X.
\]

For fixed z, the last condition has O(X^2/Z) choices: use a largest coordinate
of z as a modulus; primitivity makes the congruence on the other two
coordinates surjective. A dyadic Z-range contributes

\[
O(Z^3)\,O((X/Z)^2)\,O(X^2/Z)=O(X^4).
\]

The zero-block case contributes only O(X^3). Summing the ranges and the four
relations gives O(X^4 log(2X)), including all nonprimitive vectors.

When K>=C_1 X, the retained line-exclusion lemma removes all actual states
on line components. Apply (8.1) with d>=2 to every remaining component of
an exceptional pattern, in the original H-box. This gives total contribution

\[
\ll X^4H^{1/2}\log(2X)\log(2H).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Combining (7.2), (8.3), the bounded cut contributions, and (9.1),

\[
T\ll_\eta a^\eta\left[
X^5+X^4H^{1/2}+X^5\max(H,S)^{1/10}
\right],
\qquad K\ge C_1X.
\tag{9.2}
\]

## 10. Global exponent and the other scale range

The elementary drop estimate, for any eta>0, is

\[
Q\ll_\eta a^{1+\eta}/T^2.
\tag{10.1}
\]

The drops in the quotient block, including the exit drop, sum to at most
a/Q. At least half are at most 2a/(QT). For each such drop d,

\[
a+d=(q_i+1)b_i.
\]

Distinct remainders at a fixed d are divisors of a+d<=2a. The bound
 tau(n)<<_eta n^eta proves (10.1). If the permitted drop interval contains
no positive integer, the supposed block cannot occur.

Since H asymp Q/T and S asymp a/T^3, (10.1) gives

\[
\max(H,S)\ll_\eta a^\eta S.
\tag{10.2}
\]

A represented actual state has S>=1. Logarithms and the small exponent loss
in (10.2) can be absorbed into an arbitrarily small power of a. The local
bound consequently becomes

\[
\boxed{
T\ll_\eta a^\eta\left(X^5+X^4H^{1/2}+X^5S^{1/10}\right).
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

Use X asymp aQ^2/T^6. The first two terms give exponent 5/17 exactly as in
the preceding candidate. The new term gives

\[
T\ll_\eta a^\eta
\left(\frac{aQ^2}{T^6}\right)^5
\left(\frac a{T^3}\right)^{1/10}.
\]

Thus

\[
T^{313}\ll_\eta a^{51+10\eta}Q^{100}.
\]

Insert (10.1):

\[
\boxed{T^{513}\ll_\eta a^{151+110\eta}.}
\tag{10.4}
\]

There is no significance to the particular multiple of eta: applications
with smaller eta can always be made before combining estimates.

For the complementary range K<C_1 X, the definitions give

\[
T^{11}\ll a^2Q^3,
\qquad T^{17}\ll_\eta a^{5+3\eta}.
\]

This is smaller than the new exponent, because

\[
151/513-5/17=2/8721>0.
\]

The good-window selection is unchanged: discard sixteen final starts and
starts spoiled by a gap exceeding C_0Q/T. Each oversized gap spoils at most
sixteen starts, leaving a fixed positive fraction. All identities above
then have their stated support margin. Blocks with bounded T are harmless.

Finally choose eta sufficiently small relative to the desired epsilon and
sum the O(log a) dyadic quotient blocks. The bounded range of a is absorbed
by the constant. This yields

\[
\boxed{P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{151/513+\varepsilon}.}
\]

The critical scales are

\[
T\sim a^{151/513},\quad Q\sim a^{211/513},\quad
H\sim S\sim a^{60/513},\quad X\sim a^{29/513},\quad
K\sim a^{31/513}.
\]

The main-range condition K/X -> infinity holds, and
X^5 S^(1/10) has exponent (145+6)/513=151/513, as required.

## 11. Verification, scope, and next obstruction

The supplied checks were executed in exact arithmetic:

* One generic symbolic resolvent identity for arbitrary quartic coefficients
  and four initial moments, with later moments generated by the quartic
  recurrence. This verifies (3.2) without sampling particular measures.
* The exact polynomial for H_3, the degrees of H_3 and J, and the leading
  form of J.
* Two hundred and five actual orbit starts, each with at least ten positive states:
  the I_3 curvature identity and integrality, its Gram upper bound, (3.2),
  rational reconstruction of q and all moments through degree eight, and
  agreement of the reconstructed I_3 with the actual determinant. The targeted
  cases include I_3=1 and an orbit of length 34.
* Six formal local models with node pole orders e=2,4,6: the I_3 rational
  function has the predicted degree difference -3e/2 and nonzero leading
  coefficient. These models are explicitly NOT asserted to be actual
  fixed-dividend orbit curves.
* Two synthetic birational rational-curve parametrizations with one and two
  old infinity points. They check the degree increase from four to ten on
  adding a rational coordinate with the stated zero divisor. These likewise
  are not claimed to be orbit curves.
* Exact exponent arithmetic and critical scale comparisons.

No independent-agent review or formal verification was performed for this
refinement. The finite computations check identities and implementation;
Sections 4--10 provide the branchwise geometric argument and the uniform
count. The retained framework still needs its own external review.

The substantial change is the counting space: adding a small integer
invariant creates a curve of higher degree, rather than trying to exclude
all original quartic components. The proof no longer needs the preceding
weighted-lattice estimate. It does still use the uniform affine-curve
bound, the full pattern count, and the exceptional-pattern/line analysis.

The resulting exponent remains above 5/17, where the X^5 and complementary
scale contributions meet. This note does not improve those two terms or
prove the original logarithmic conjecture.
