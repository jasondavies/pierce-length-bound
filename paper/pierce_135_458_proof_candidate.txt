# Conic arithmetic in the Pierce remainder iteration

## Status and claim

Research proof candidate, 19 October 2026. This note extends the uploaded
`pierce_18_61_proof_candidate.md`; it is not an externally reviewed paper or
formally verified proof. No priority claim is made. The new arguments are the
rational reconstruction lemma and the arithmetic treatment of parabolic
conics. The moment, minor, line-exclusion, and lattice-incidence lemmas listed
in Section 1 are retained from the preceding candidate, not replaced by
computational evidence.

The resulting proposed bound is

\[
\boxed{P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{135/458+\varepsilon}},
\qquad 135/458=0.2947598253275109\ldots.
\]

It improves the preceding candidate by

\[
18/61-135/458=9/27938.
\]

It does not resolve the original logarithmic-squared conjecture.

The main local estimate, with an arbitrarily small power of a absorbing
logarithms, is

\[
T\ll_\eta a^\eta\left(X^5+X^4H^{1/2}+X^{44/9}H^{1/3}\right).
\tag{0.1}
\]

The first term counts nonparabolic conics and most parabolic conics using
arithmetic, the second counts exceptional parabolic patterns, and the third
counts curve components of degree at least three. The third term determines
the final exponent.

## 1. Retained framework and precise dependencies

Write

\[
a=q_i b_i+b_{i+1},\qquad k_i=q_{i+1}-q_i>0,
\]

until the first zero remainder. The positive remainders strictly decrease and
the quotients strictly increase. A quotient block is Q <= q_i < 2Q, and T is
its number of states. A fixed positive fraction of its starts have their next
sixteen quotient gaps at most

\[
H=C_0Q/T
\]

for an absolute C_0. The final sixteen starts are discarded. This is the
support margin used throughout; all identities involving finite moments
below are applied only to these starts. Put

\[
X=C_2aH^6/Q^4,\qquad K=Q^4/(aH^5),
\]

where C_2 bounds the minors below. If a selected start exists then X >= 1.
The scales satisfy H << a and X << a^7; these crude bounds are enough for all
coefficient-height arguments.

Here are the retained lemmas from the 18/61 note, with section references to
that note.

1. **Finite positive moments (Sections 1 and 4).** At a start there are positive
   finite measures mu and nu, with
   \[
   (q+x)\nu=\mu,\quad \mu(1)=\mu(x)=1,\quad \nu(1)=b/a.
   \]
   Write s_j=q_(i+j)-q_i and F_j(x)=product_(v<j)(x-s_v). Then
   \[
   \nu(F_j)=b_{i+j}/a,\qquad \mu(F_j)=1.
   \]
   Raw moments through degree six depend polynomially on the first five gaps.
   The weighted Hankel matrix is positive definite on the degrees used here.

2. **Integral minor pattern (Sections 2--3).** Form the 3 by 5 matrix with rows
   \[
   d_j=b_{i+j}-b_{i+j+1},\quad k_j,\quad
   r_j=k_j(s_j+s_{j+1})+k_{j+1},\quad 0\le j\le4.
   \]
   Its ten maximal minors p have height at most X. Set
   \[
   A=p_{012},\ A'=p_{123},\ A''=p_{234},\ U=p_{023},\ V=-p_{013},
   \ U'=p_{134},\ V'=-p_{124}.
   \]
   A,A',A'' are positive integers. There are O(X^5) full minor patterns,
   including nonprimitive patterns. One may prove the latter by a maximal
   pivot m: the remaining coordinates give vectors u,v modulo m with
   u cross v=0. For g=gcd(m,u_1,u_2,u_3), the number of v residues is m g^2;
   summing yields O(X^5).

3. **Quartics and curves (Sections 4--5).** Define
   \[
   \rho=A'/A,\ u=U/A,\ v=V/A,\qquad
   \rho'=A''/A',\ u'=U'/A',\ v'=V'/A',
   \]
   and
   \[
   c=(\rho,-\rho-u,u-v,v-1,1),
   \]
   with c' defined similarly. Exactly rho>0,u>0,v<0, so every c_j is nonzero,
   c_0,c_2,c_4>0 and c_1,c_3<0, with c_3<-1. Put
   \[
   P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c_jF_j(x),\qquad
   Z(x)=xR(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c'_jF_{j+1}(x).
   \]
   P and Z have degrees four and five, and
   \[
   \nu(x^mP)=\nu(x^mZ)=0\quad(0\le m\le3).
   \]
   The two linear relations determine k_0 and k_4 from k_1,k_2,k_3. The first
   quadratic is
   \[
   f=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j(s_j^2+k_j)=0.
   \]
   The next quadratic determines k_5; substituting it defines a cubic D with
   homogeneous part
   \[
   D_3=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^3.
   \]
   At actual states D=nu(P^2)>0 and
   \[
   A\operatorname{Res}(P,Z)=aD^4.
   \]
   Thus actual gap points lie on f=Omega=0, where
   \[
   \Omega=A\operatorname{Res}(P,Z)-aD^4.
   \]
   The resultant has gap degree ten, f is absolutely irreducible of degree
   two, and f and Omega are coprime. Their common zero locus has total curve
   degree at most 24. Any isolated contributions have bounded size.

4. **Raw determinant and normalization (Sections 7 and 10).** With
   \[
   H_4=\det[\mu(x^{j+k})]_{j,k<4},
   \]
   one has
   \[
   H_4=\prod_{0\le j<l<4}(s_l-s_j)+E,
   \qquad \deg E\le5
   \tag{1.1}
   \]
   as a polynomial in nearby quotient gaps. Also
   \[
   A P(-q)=aH_4.
   \tag{1.2}
   \]
   Fixed pattern and five gaps consequently represent at most four states.

5. **Line exclusion (Sections 6--7).** When K >= C_1 X, with an absolute C_1,
   no selected actual state lies on a line component of f=Omega=0. The
   complementary range satisfies T <<_eta a^(5/17+eta).

6. **Pattern lattices (Sections 8--9).** The primitive rank-three lattice
   \[
   L_p=\operatorname{rowspan}_{\mathbb R}(M)\cap\mathbb Z^5
   \]
   contains the five-gap vector. A reduced basis gives integer coordinates
   of size O(H/lambda_1(L_p)). If N(Y,R) counts primitive rank-three
   sublattices of Z^5 of determinant at most Y with shortest length at most R,
   then
   \[
   N(Y,R)\ll Y^4R^3\quad(1\le R\ll Y^{1/3}).
   \tag{1.3}
   \]
   The note proves this uniformly, including short-vector and common-divisor
   cases.

The new proof does not assume that the full algebraic curve consists of
actual orbit points. Additional identities holding only at actual points
are either used as further bounded-degree cuts or proved to hold identically
on a parametrized component before taking their degrees.

## 2. Rational reconstruction from two quartics

### Lemma 2.1

For fixed P of degree four and Z of degree five, with Res(P,Z) nonzero, the
linear functional on polynomials of degree at most eight satisfying

\[
\ell(x^jP)=\ell(x^jZ)=0\quad(0\le j\le3)
\]

is unique up to scale.

**Proof.** The eight row polynomials are linearly independent. A dependence
would give U P+V Z=0 with deg U,deg V <=3. Coprimality forces P to divide V,
which is impossible unless V=0; then U=0. The ambient polynomial space has
dimension nine. Thus its annihilator has dimension one. This reasoning works
over any field of characteristic zero, including Q(t).

Let h_0,...,h_8 be a nonzero cofactor kernel vector of the 8 by 9 coefficient
matrix. They can be chosen polynomially in the coefficients of P,Z. At an
actual state h_0 is nonzero, and m_j=h_j/h_0=nu(x^j)/nu(1).

The equations mu(1)=mu(x)=1 give

\[
q\nu(1)+\nu(x)=1,\qquad q\nu(x)+\nu(x^2)=1.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
q=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1-1},\qquad
b=\frac{a}{q+m_1}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

All denominators are nonzero at actual states. Indeed positive definiteness
gives m_2>m_1^2. If m_1=1, the two displayed equations would force m_2=1.
Also q+m_1=a/b>0. The nonzero resultant follows independently from D>0 and
the fixed-dividend identity.

When P,Z depend rationally on a parameter t, (2.1) reconstructs q,b as rational
functions of t, outside a bounded set of algebraic exceptional values. If a
kernel coordinate or denominator vanishes identically in a way that makes
this reconstruction unavailable, that component has no actual states.

All degrees here are bounded absolutely. If the rational coefficients of P,Z
have numerators and denominators bounded by a fixed power of a, the same is
true for the rational functions obtained by these fixed-size determinants,
after reduction. This also follows from fixed-degree subresultant formulas.

## 3. An elementary integer-value lemma

### Lemma 3.1

Suppose F,G in Z[t] are coprime over Q, G is nonconstant, and their degrees are
at most d. Put R=Res(F,G), which is a nonzero integer. Then

\[
\#\{t\in\mathbb Z:G(t)\ne0,\ F(t)/G(t)\in\mathbb Z\}
\le 2d\,\tau(|R|).
\tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** The Sylvester adjugate yields integer polynomial coefficients in a
Bezout identity U F+V G=R. At an integral value F(t)/G(t), G(t) divides F(t),
and hence G(t) divides R. For each signed divisor e of R, the nonconstant
polynomial G(t)-e has at most d roots. Sum over the signed divisors.

In particular, a proper nonzero rational function of bounded degree and
height at most a^C has O_(C,d,eta)(a^eta) integer values at integer parameters,
for every eta>0. After clearing denominators and cancelling the polynomial
gcd, the resulting integer polynomials still have height bounded by a fixed
power of a. A fixed-degree resultant has the same property. The elementary
estimate tau(n) <<_delta n^delta then applies.

For the height statement, no factorization-height theorem with variable
degree is necessary: for fixed degree, Cauchy's root bound bounds every root
by a fixed power of the original height, and Gauss's lemma bounds a primitive
factor's leading coefficient by the original leading coefficient. Its other
coefficients are consequently bounded by a fixed power as well.

## 4. Conic planes and an integer parameter for parabolas

Fix one degree-two component over Q and one coefficient pattern. If it has at
most two actual gap points, its contribution is already O(1). Otherwise take
three such points. An absolutely irreducible conic has no three collinear
points, so these points determine its plane. The plane equation can be chosen
with integer coefficients of size O(H^3), by taking a cross product of the
two difference vectors and then the constant term.

Project away a coordinate whose plane coefficient is nonzero. The remaining
two coordinates x,y of an actual gap point are integers bounded by H. The
third coordinate is an affine rational function of them. Restrict the
retained quadratic f to this plane and clear denominators. Because f contains
the conic but no plane, the resulting polynomial is a nonzero scalar multiple
of the irreducible conic equation. Its integer coefficients have height at
most a^C for an absolute C. Here H << a, X << a^7 and the coefficients of f
are rational functions of the bounded minor pattern with bounded degrees.

A bounded unimodular change of x,y ensures that the first square coefficient
is nonzero. Write the equation as

\[
\alpha x^2+\beta xy+\gamma y^2+d x+e y+f_0=0,
\qquad \alpha\ne0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let Delta=beta^2-4 alpha gamma. If Delta=0, the conic is a parabola (one point
at infinity counted twice). Set

\[
J=2\beta d-4\alpha e,\qquad E=d^2-4\alpha f_0,
\qquad t=2\alpha x+\beta y+d.
\]

Then

\[
t^2=J y+E.
\]

Irreducibility over the algebraic closure implies J != 0; otherwise the conic
factors over that field. Thus

\[
y(t)=\frac{t^2-E}{J},\qquad
x(t)=\frac{t-\beta y(t)-d}{2\alpha}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every actual integer gap point gives an integer t. The parameter is injective
on the affine conic. All three original gap coordinates and all node
coordinates are rational polynomials in t of degree at most two. Their
coefficients have numerators and denominators at most a^C, as does the bound
on |t| at actual points.

Write

\[
s_j(t)=z_jt^2+w_jt+e_j,\qquad z_0=w_0=e_0=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The vector of leading central-gap coordinates is nonzero. The map from those
coordinates to s_1,s_2,s_3 is invertible; consequently not all the z_j are zero.

If a Q-irreducible degree-two component is geometrically reducible, its
rational points lie on the intersection of its two conjugate lines, giving
at most one point. Such components can be included in the O(1) contribution.

## 5. Parabolas with distinct node directions: actual states have a divisor bound

### Lemma 5.1

For a fixed coefficient pattern and an absolutely irreducible parabolic conic
component of f=Omega=0, suppose the five leading node coefficients
z_0,...,z_4 in (4.3) are pairwise distinct. Then the number of actual orbit
states on that component with gaps at most H is O_eta(a^eta), uniformly in the
pattern and component.

**Proof.** Use the integer parameter t from Section 4. Form the rational
functions q(t),b(t) from Lemma 2.1. If Res(P,Z) vanishes identically on the
component, the fixed-dividend identity forces D to vanish there and there are
no actual states. Otherwise the cofactor reconstruction applies. Its degree
is bounded absolutely, and every rational function used below has height
bounded by a fixed power of a. Exceptional denominator values have bounded
cardinality and do not contribute unboundedly; the actual moment conditions
in fact rule out the reconstruction poles.

First impose the actual-state identity

\[
A P_t(-q(t))=aH_4(t).
\tag{5.1}
\]

If it is not an identity in Q(t), its numerator is a nonzero polynomial of
bounded degree. There are O(1) actual parameter values, which proves the
lemma in this case. We may therefore suppose (5.1) is an identity.

The polynomial P_t is monic of degree four in its argument and has
coefficients in Q[t]. Equation (5.1) is thus a monic polynomial equation for
the rational function q(t) over Q[t]. It follows that q(t) is a polynomial.
An elementary proof is to put q=N/D in lowest terms in Q[t]: after multiplying
by D^4, the monic equation implies D divides N^4, so D is constant.

The raw determinant identity (1.1) gives

\[
\deg_t H_4(t)=12,
\qquad [t^{12}]H_4(t)=
\prod_{0\le j<l<4}(z_l-z_j)\ne0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed the leading Vandermonde has degree 12, while the lower gap-degree
terms have t-degree at most 10. If deg q <=2, the left side of (5.1) has degree
at most eight, impossible. If deg q=m>2, its degree is exactly 4m, since the
quartic is monic and each s_j has degree at most two. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\deg q=3.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Next impose the quartic remainder relation. Define rational functions

\[
r_0(t)=b(t),\qquad r_{j+1}(t)=a-(q(t)+s_j(t))r_j(t)
\quad(0\le j<4).
\]

At actual states r_j=b_(i+j), so

\[
\sum_{j=0}^4c_jr_j(t)=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

If (5.4) is not a rational-function identity, it again contributes only O(1)
actual parameters. Suppose it is an identity. Put

\[
Q_j(t)=\prod_{l<j}(q(t)+s_l(t)),\qquad
S_0=0,\quad S_{j+1}=1-(q+s_j)S_j.
\]

Then r_j=(-1)^j Q_j b+aS_j. Consequently

\[
b(t)=-a\frac{W(t)}{V(t)},\qquad
V=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j(-1)^jQ_j=P_t(-q(t)),\quad
W=\sum_{j=0}^4c_jS_j.
\tag{5.5}
\]

If gamma is the nonzero cubic leading coefficient of q, then every q+s_j
has degree three and leading coefficient gamma. Since c_4=1,

\[
\deg V=12,\quad [t^{12}]V=\gamma^4,
\qquad
\deg W=9,\quad [t^9]W=-\gamma^3.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus b(t) is nonzero and proper, with degree difference -3 even after common
factors are cancelled. In lowest terms its denominator is nonconstant.
Every actual remainder b(t) is an integer. Lemma 3.1 now bounds the number of
actual integer parameters by O_eta(a^eta). Because t determines the gap point
and the reconstruction determines q and b, this counts states themselves.
This proves the lemma.

**Logical point.** Equations (5.1) and (5.4) were not assumed to hold at every
point of the algebraic conic. Each was explicitly treated as an additional
bounded-degree cut unless it is an identity. The degree argument is used
only in the identity cases.

## 6. The remaining parabolic patterns are a smaller set

The leading homogeneous pieces of the retained equations are

\[
f_2=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^2,\qquad
D_3=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^3.
\]

For any parabola component, the resultant has t-degree at most 20, because
its gap degree is ten. The identity A Res(P,Z)=aD^4 forces deg_t D <=5.
Therefore (4.3) satisfies

\[
\sum_{j=0}^4c_jz_j^m=0\quad(m=0,1,2,3).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The equations for m=0,1 are the coefficient sum and the retained first linear
relation; those for m=2,3 follow from f and the degree comparison just made.

If two of the z_j coincide, there are at most four distinct values. Group
identical values and apply the Vandermonde matrix to (6.1). Each cluster has
coefficient sum zero. Since all c_j are nonzero, there are no singleton
clusters. A single cluster would give every z_j=0, which is impossible for a
genuine parabola. Thus there are two clusters of sizes two and three.

The signs and formulas for c allow only four vanishing pair sums:

| Pair | Equivalent minor relation |
|---|---|
| c_0+c_3=0 | A'+V-A=0 |
| c_1+c_2=0 | A'+V=0 |
| c_2+c_3=0 | U-A=0 |
| c_1+c_4=0 | A-A'-U=0 |

Each relation is one zero maximal minor after an integer unimodular column
change with bounded coefficients. If v_0,...,v_4 are the original columns,
the four determinants are respectively

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\det(v_1,v_2+v_0,v_3-v_0),\\
&\det(v_1,v_2+v_0,v_3),\\
&\det(v_0,v_2,v_3+v_1),\\
&\det(v_0+v_1,v_2,-v_1-v_3).
\end{aligned}
\]

In each case the three displayed columns extend to a bounded unimodular
change of all five columns, for example by retaining appropriate original
columns v_0 or v_1 and v_4. Heights change by an absolute factor only.

### Lemma 6.1

There are O(X^4 log(2X)) integral decomposable three-vectors of height at most
X having a specified maximal coordinate zero. This includes every common
factor of the coordinates.

**Proof.** Hodge duality turns such a vector into an integral decomposable
bivector w in five coordinates, with w_45=0 after a permutation. Put

\[
\mathbf a=(w_{14},w_{24},w_{34}),\qquad
\mathbf b=(w_{15},w_{25},w_{35}),\qquad
\mathbf t=(w_{23},-w_{13},w_{12}).
\]

The Pluecker relations give

\[
\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=0,\qquad
\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf a=\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf b=0.
\]

If a=b=0 there are O(X^3) possibilities. Otherwise write

\[
\mathbf a=r\mathbf z,\qquad \mathbf b=s\mathbf z,
\]

with z primitive integral, r,s integral and not both zero. If
Z=||z||_infinity, then 1<=Z<<X, |r|,|s|<<X/Z, and z dot t=0.
For fixed z, the number of t in the X-box is O(X^2/Z). To see this use a
largest coordinate of z, of absolute value Z, as the modulus. The resulting
congruence on the other two t-coordinates is surjective, because z is
primitive; the final t-coordinate is determined.

In one dyadic Z-range there are O(Z^3) possible primitive z, O((X/Z)^2)
choices for r,s, and O(X^2/Z) choices for t. Their product is O(X^4).
Summing the O(log(2X)) ranges proves the assertion.

Consequently the parabolic components not covered by Lemma 5.1 occur in at
most O(X^4 log(2X)) coefficient patterns. The uniform degree-two curve bound
counts their integer gap points by O(H^(1/2) log(2H)) per component. The total
number of components per pattern is bounded by 24. The bounded multiplicity
from (1.2) changes the constant only. Their total contribution is therefore

\[
\ll X^4H^{1/2}\log(2X)\log(2H).
\tag{6.2}
\]

These patterns are overcounted: a vanishing pair need not actually support a
parabola. No assertion of sufficiency is used.

## 7. Nonparabolic conics have a uniform divisor bound

Use the bounded-height plane equation (4.1). In this case

\[
\Delta=\beta^2-4\alpha\gamma\ne0.
\]

Put

\[
\Lambda=\beta d-2\alpha e,\quad
U=2\alpha x+\beta y+d,\quad V=\Delta y+\Lambda,
\]

and

\[
N=\Lambda^2+\Delta(4\alpha f_0-d^2).
\]

On the conic,

\[
V^2-\Delta U^2=N.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The transformation is injective, since Delta and alpha are nonzero. Also
N != 0: if N=0, (7.1) factors into two lines over the algebraic closure,
contradicting geometric irreducibility. Coefficients, |N|, and the bounds on
|U|,|V| are all at most a fixed power of a.

Here is a self-contained uniform norm estimate sufficient for (7.1):

\[
\#\{(p,q)\in\mathbb Z^2:p^2-Eq^2=N,
 |p|,|q|\le B\}
\ll \tau(|N|)\log(2B(1+\sqrt{|E|})),
\tag{7.2}
\]

for E != 0 and N != 0. An absolute factor suffices in this statement.

For a positive square E=m^2, factor (p-mq)(p+mq)=N. Otherwise write
E=d f^2 with signed squarefree d != 1. For each solution put

\[
g=\gcd(p,fq),\qquad r=p/g,\ s=fq/g,\ n=N/g^2.
\]

Then r^2-ds^2=n, gcd(r,s)=1, and gcd(s,n)=1. The residue r/s modulo |n|
satisfies t^2=d mod |n|. A prime-power calculation gives at most
4*2^(omega(|n|)) such classes (modulus one is harmless).

For two primitive solutions in one residue class, the ratio

\[
\frac{r+s\sqrt d}{r'+s'\sqrt d}
=\frac{rr'-dss'+(sr'-rs')\sqrt d}{n}
\]

belongs to Z[sqrt(d)] and has norm one. If d<0 there are at most four such
units, from h^2+|d|j^2=1. If d>1, any positive unit greater than one in this
ring is at least 2+sqrt(3): its integral real part is (epsilon+epsilon^-1)/2
and is at least two. The two real embeddings of r+s sqrt(d) have absolute
value at most L=B(1+sqrt(|E|)); their product is the nonzero integer n.
Hence its nonzero absolute value is between 1/L and L. Multiplicative
separation gives O(log(2L)) solutions in one residue class, including both
signs. Finally

\[
\sum_{g^2\mid |N|}2^{\omega(|N|/g^2)}=\tau(|N|),
\]

as is checked prime power by prime power. This proves (7.2).

It follows that each nonparabolic conic contributes O_eta(a^eta) states.
Together with Lemma 5.1, the at most two-point cases, and geometrically
reducible conics, these contributions sum to

\[
O_\eta(a^\eta X^5).
\tag{7.3}
\]

## 8. Higher-degree curves in their pattern lattices

The external counting theorem is the same as in the preceding note: for an
affine curve C over Q, irreducible over Q, of degree d in fixed dimension n,

\[
N_{\rm aff}(C,B)\ll_n d^3B^{1/d}(\log B+d).
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is Corollary 2.2 of Cluckers--Debes--Hendel--Nguyen--Vermeulen,
*Improvements on dimension growth results and effective Hilbert's
irreducibility theorem*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 13 (2025), e153,
DOI 10.1017/fms.2025.10096. It attributes the result to
Castryck--Cluckers--Dittmann--Nguyen (2020). Its constant is independent of
coefficient height.

For degree at least three, lattice coordinates from Section 1 give the bound

\[
\ll \left(H/\lambda_1(L_p)\right)^{1/3}\log(2H)
\]

per pattern, because all component degrees are at most 24. Actual patterns
contain a nonzero gap vector of length at most sqrt(5) H, so the ratio
H/lambda_1 is bounded below by an absolute positive constant. Rounding the
box size changes only constants.

The same short-vector incidence estimate (1.3), with weight 1/3 instead of
1/2, gives

\[
\sum_{\substack{L\text{ primitive}\\ \det L\le Y}}
\lambda_1(L)^{-1/3}
\ll
\sum_{R\ll Y^{1/3}}Y^4R^{3-1/3}
\ll Y^{44/9}.
\]

For a nonprimitive minor vector p=g p_0 of height at most X, its lattice has
determinant ||p_0||_2 <= sqrt(10) X/g. There are two primitive orientations
per lattice, and sum_g g^(-44/9) converges. Thus

\[
\boxed{\sum_p\lambda_1(L_p)^{-1/3}\ll X^{44/9}.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The higher-degree contribution is therefore

\[
\ll H^{1/3}X^{44/9}\log(2H).
\tag{8.3}
\]

The invertible rational change from lattice coordinates to central gap
coordinates preserves curve degrees. Coefficient uniformity in (8.1) means
that no height bound on that change is required.

## 9. Local estimate and exponent

In the main range K >= C_1 X, no selected actual state lies on a line
component by the retained line-exclusion lemma. Equations (6.2), (7.3), and
(8.3) give, with any eta>0,

\[
\boxed{
T\ll_\eta a^\eta
\left(X^5+X^4H^{1/2}+X^{44/9}H^{1/3}\right).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

The fixed support convention and good-window selection retain at least T/2
starts. Fixed pattern and five gaps represent at most four starts; those
constants are already incorporated into (9.1).

The elementary complementary estimate is

\[
Q\ll_\eta a^{1+\eta}/T^2.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Indeed the remainder drops within the quotient block, including its last
exit drop, sum to at most a/Q. At least half are at most 2a/(QT), and

\[
a+d_i=(q_i+1)b_i.
\]

At any fixed drop, distinct remainders are divisors of a+d_i <=2a. The usual
elementary bound tau(n) <<_eta n^eta proves (9.2). A drop interval containing
no positive integer cannot occur for such a block.

Substitute H asymp Q/T and X asymp aQ^2/T^6 into (9.1). Some term contributes
at least a fixed positive fraction of the right side; treat them separately.
Arbitrarily small powers a^eta below may be renamed between uses.

* From X^5:
  T^31 << a^(5+eta) Q^10 and hence
  T^51 << a^(15+11eta). The exponent is 5/17.

* From X^4 H^(1/2):
  T^51 << a^(8+2eta) Q^17 and hence
  T^85 << a^(25+19eta). The exponent is also 5/17.

* From X^(44/9) H^(1/3):
  \[
  T^{276}\ll_\eta a^{44+9\eta}Q^{91},
  \qquad
  \boxed{T^{458}\ll_\eta a^{135+100\eta}.}
  \]
  This yields 135/458.

The complementary scale K<C_1X implies

\[
T^{11}\ll a^2Q^3,\qquad T^{17}\ll_\eta a^{5+3\eta},
\]

again with exponent 5/17. The strict gap is

\[
135/458-5/17=5/7786>0.
\]

Choose eta sufficiently small relative to a prescribed epsilon and sum the
O(log a) quotient blocks. The extra logarithm is absorbed into a^epsilon.
All bounded T and bounded a cases are absorbed into the constant. This gives

\[
\boxed{P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{135/458+\varepsilon}.}
\]

At the final balance,

\[
T\sim a^{135/458},\quad Q\sim a^{188/458},\quad
H\sim a^{53/458},\quad X\sim a^{24/458},\quad
K\sim a^{29/458}.
\]

In particular K/X grows like a^(5/458), as required. The leading local term
has exponent

\[
(44/9)(24/458)+(1/3)(53/458)=135/458.
\]

## 10. Verification and limits of the verification

The accompanying scripts were executed in exact rational arithmetic and
symbolic polynomial arithmetic. They check:

1. Rational reconstruction from the eight orthogonality equations on 200
   actual orbit starts, all with at least ten positive states. They recover
   both q and b, verify positive variance and mu(1)=mu(x)=1, reproduce four
   remainder steps, and verify the quartic remainder relation.
2. Three generic parabola-parametrization identities.
3. A synthetic rational parabola lying on the first quadratic, with distinct
   leading nodes. Its raw determinant has degree twelve and leading
   coefficient twelve. Its reconstructed quotient is a genuine rational
   function, of numerator degree eight and denominator degree five, so the
   monic normalization cannot be an identity for a fixed nonzero dividend.
   This is an algebraic test, not a claimed family of actual orbit states.
4. The proper-rational degree calculation (degrees twelve and nine) on an
   explicit polynomial family, plus exact exponent arithmetic.
5. Four generic determinant identities for the exceptional minor relations,
   and the generic completion-of-squares identity for conics.
6. Eighty coprime proper rational functions, at 161 integer parameters each,
   testing the resultant divisibility condition at all their integral
   values (77 such values in this fixed test set).

The determinant identities and parametrization identities are symbolic
checks. The orbit and rational-function samples are regression tests.
Neither kind of computation proves the uniform counting estimates on its
own. Sections 2--9 give the arguments; Section 1 states the exact dependencies
on the preceding proof candidate. No independent-agent proof audits or formal
verification were performed for this refinement.

The remaining largest contribution comes from degree-three curve components.
The argument does not assert that those components attain the cubic-root
count, nor that the parabolic exception conditions are sufficient for an
actual component to exist.
