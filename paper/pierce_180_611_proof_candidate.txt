# A degree-parity refinement for the Pierce remainder iteration

## Status and claim

Research proof candidate, 19 October 2026. This note extends
`pierce_135_458_proof_candidate.md` and its retained dependency
`pierce_18_61_proof_candidate.md`. It has not received external mathematical
review or formal verification. No priority claim is made.

The new argument is a parity lemma for the degrees of the fixed-pattern
curve components. It is proved below using normalization of curves and
orders of vanishing. The conic arithmetic, bounded pattern multiplicity,
line exclusion, minor counts, and short-vector lattice incidence estimate
are retained from the named dependency notes. Numerical checks do not
replace those dependencies.

Subject to that retained framework, the resulting bound is

\[
\boxed{P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{180/611+\varepsilon}},
\qquad 180/611=0.2945990180032733\ldots.
\]

The exact improvement over the preceding candidate is

\[
135/458-180/611=45/279838.
\]

The original conjecture \(P(a,b)=O((\log a)^2)\) is not resolved.

The improved local estimate is

\[
\boxed{
T\ll_\eta a^\eta
\left(X^5+X^4H^{1/2}+X^{59/12}H^{1/4}\right).
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

The final term previously had the form \(X^{44/9}H^{1/3}\). The change
comes from proving that **nonexceptional patterns have no odd-degree
geometric curve components at all**, not from improving a general bound
for the integer points of a cubic.

## 1. Retained setup and dependencies

Write the finite remainder orbit as

\[
a=q_i b_i+b_{i+1},\qquad k_i=q_{i+1}-q_i>0,
\]

until the first zero remainder. The quotients strictly increase. In a
quotient block \(Q\le q_i<2Q\) with \(T\) states, a fixed positive
fraction of the starts have their next sixteen quotient gaps bounded by

\[
H=C_0Q/T.
\]

The last sixteen starts of the block are discarded. All local moment
identities below use this support margin. Put

\[
X=C_2aH^6/Q^4,\qquad K=Q^4/(aH^5),
\]

with fixed sufficiently large constants where required. A represented
pattern has a positive integer minor, hence \(X\ge1\).

Here are the exact retained statements used by the refinement.

1. Form the integer 3-by-5 matrix whose rows are, for \(j=0,\ldots,4\),
   \[
   d_j=b_{i+j}-b_{i+j+1},\quad k_j,\quad
   r_j=k_j(s_j+s_{j+1})+k_{j+1},\qquad s_j=q_{i+j}-q_i.
   \]
   Its ten maximal minors \(\mathbf p\) have height at most \(X\).
   There are \(O(X^5)\) such full patterns, including nonprimitive vectors.

2. Set
   \[
   A=p_{012},\ A'=p_{123},\ A''=p_{234},\quad
   U=p_{023},\ V=-p_{013},\ U'=p_{134},\ V'=-p_{124}.
   \]
   The first three are positive integers. With
   \[
   \rho=A'/A,\quad u=U/A,\quad v=V/A,
   \]
   the first quartic's Newton coefficients are
   \[
   (c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)
   =(\rho,-\rho-u,u-v,v-1,1),
   \]
   and exactly \(\rho>0,u>0,v<0\). Thus every \(c_j\ne0\).

3. The two consecutive linear moment relations determine \(k_0,k_4\)
   linearly from \((k_1,k_2,k_3)\). The five nodes \(s_0,\ldots,s_4\)
   are homogeneous linear functions of these three coordinates, with
   \(s_0=0\), and
   \[
   \sum_{j=0}^4c_j=0,\qquad \sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j=0.
   \tag{1.1}
   \]
   The three functions \(s_1,s_2,s_3\) are independent linear coordinates.

4. Let
   \[
   F_j(x)=\prod_{v<j}(x-s_v),\quad
   P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c_jF_j(x),\quad
   Z(x)=xR(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4c'_jF_{j+1}(x).
   \]
   The next quadratic determines \(k_5\). After substituting it, define
   \[
   f=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j(s_j^2+k_j),
   \]
   and the cubic norm polynomial
   \[
   D=\sum_{j=0}^4c_j\{s_j^3+3s_j^2+3s_j(1+k_j)
       +(1+k_j)^2+k_{j+1}\}.
   \]
   Their leading homogeneous terms are
   \[
   f_2=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^2,\qquad
   D_3=\sum_{j=0}^4c_js_j^3.
   \tag{1.2}
   \]
   Set \(\mathcal R=\operatorname{Res}_x(P,Z)\). It has degree ten and
   \[
   \mathcal R_{10}
   =\kappa\prod_{0\le j<\ell\le4}(s_\ell-s_j),
   \qquad \kappa=c_0c_1c_2c_3\ne0.
   \tag{1.3}
   \]
   Every actual state satisfies
   \[
   f=0,\qquad A\mathcal R=aD^4,\qquad D>0.
   \tag{1.4}
   \]
   The two polynomials \(f\) and \(\Omega=A\mathcal R-aD^4\) have no
   common surface component; their intersection has total curve degree at
   most 24. Bounded isolated contributions can be absorbed in \(O(1)\)
   per pattern.

5. If \(K\ge C_1X\), no selected actual state lies on any line component.
   In the complementary range the drop estimate gives
   \(T\ll_\eta a^{5/17+\eta}\).

6. A nonexceptional parabolic conic, meaning that its five leading node
   directions are distinct, contributes \(O_\eta(a^\eta)\) actual states
   per fixed pattern. This is Lemma 5.1 of the 135/458 note, including its
   bounded-height and exceptional-denominator cases. It uses rational
   reconstruction from the two quartics and the integrality of the
   reconstructed remainder. A reminder is given in Section 6 below.

7. The primitive rank-three lattice
   \[
   L_{\mathbf p}=\operatorname{rowspan}_{\mathbb R}(M)\cap\mathbb Z^5
   \]
   contains the five-gap vector. If \(\lambda_1\) is its shortest-vector
   length, a reduced basis gives integer coordinates of size
   \(O(H/\lambda_1)\). This change is invertible on the three independent
   gap coordinates, hence preserves curve degrees. If \(N(Y,R)\) counts
   primitive rank-three lattices of determinant at most \(Y\) with
   shortest length at most \(R\), then
   \[
   N(Y,R)\ll Y^4R^3\quad(1\le R\ll Y^{1/3}).
   \tag{1.5}
   \]
   These are Sections 8--9 of the 18/61 note.

8. Fixed pattern and five gaps represent at most four orbit states, using
   the determinant identity \(A P(-q_i)=aH_4\), which is a quartic equation
   for \(q_i\). Quotients strictly increase along a genuine orbit.

The present note proves the new geometric restriction before applying
these counting inputs.

## 2. Exact definition of exceptional patterns

Call an actual coefficient pattern **exceptional** if some pair satisfies
\(c_j+c_\ell=0\), \(j\ne\ell\). Otherwise call it nonexceptional.
This is an exact classification; it does not presume any distribution of
patterns.

The signs \(\rho>0,u>0,v<0\) and the formulas for \(c\) show that only
four pairs can vanish:

| Pair | Coefficient sum | Equivalent minor relation |
|---|---|---|
| (0,3) | \(\rho+v-1\) | \(A'+V-A=0\) |
| (1,2) | \(-\rho-v\) | \(A'+V=0\) |
| (2,3) | \(u-1\) | \(U-A=0\) |
| (1,4) | \(1-\rho-u\) | \(A-A'-U=0\) |

The remaining pair sums are
\(-u,\rho+u-v,\rho+1,-\rho-u+v-1,u-v+1,v\), which are all nonzero.

Each displayed minor relation is a single maximal minor made zero by a
bounded unimodular change of the five matrix columns. For original
columns \(v_0,\ldots,v_4\), suitable three-column determinants are

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\det(v_1,v_2+v_0,v_3-v_0),\\
&\det(v_1,v_2+v_0,v_3),\\
&\det(v_0,v_2,v_3+v_1),\\
&\det(v_0+v_1,v_2,-v_1-v_3).
\end{aligned}
\]

The selected columns extend to an integral basis change by retaining
appropriate original columns and \(v_4\). Heights change by a constant
factor only.

For later use,

\[
\boxed{\#\{\text{exceptional patterns of height at most }X\}
       \ll X^4\log(2X).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

An elementary proof is included in Section 5. No sufficiency of the four
relations for supporting a particular curve is assumed.

## 3. New parity theorem

### Theorem 3.1

Fix a nonexceptional pattern in the setup of Section 1. Every geometrically
irreducible affine curve component of \(f=\Omega=0\) has **even degree**.
More precisely, on the normalization of its projective closure, the
pullback divisor of the hyperplane at infinity has even multiplicity at
every point.

Consequently the same is true of the degree of every irreducible component
over \(\mathbb Q\): its geometric component degrees add. In particular,
there are no degree-three components for a nonexceptional pattern.

### 3.1 Homogenization

Work over an algebraic closure of characteristic zero. Denote the
homogenizing coordinate by \(w\). Homogenize \(f,D,\mathcal R\) to
degrees 2, 3 and 10, writing the resulting forms as
\(\widehat f,\widehat D,\widehat{\mathcal R}\).
The fixed-dividend equation homogenizes to degree twelve as

\[
\boxed{
A w^2\widehat{\mathcal R}=a\widehat D^{\,4}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(\overline C\) be the projective closure of an irreducible affine
curve component. It is not contained in \(w=0\). Both the homogeneous
quadratic and (3.1) vanish on \(\overline C\).

### 3.2 At infinity the node values cannot collide

At a point of \(\overline C\cap\{w=0\}\), evaluate the five homogeneous
linear node functions, giving values \(z_0,\ldots,z_4\), up to a common
nonzero scalar. Here \(z_0=0\) and not all values vanish, because
\(s_1,s_2,s_3\) are independent coordinates.

The equations at this point imply

\[
\sum_{j=0}^4c_jz_j^m=0\qquad(m=0,1,2,3).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For \(m=0,1\) use (1.1); for \(m=2\), use \(\widehat f=0\). For
\(m=3\), equation (3.1) at \(w=0\) forces
\(\widehat D=D_3(z)=0\), since \(a\ne0\).

Suppose two node values coincide. Group equal values into clusters, with
at most four distinct cluster values. The first as many equations in
(3.2) as there are clusters form an invertible Vandermonde system for the
cluster coefficient sums. Thus each cluster has coefficient sum zero.

No cluster can have size one because all \(c_j\ne0\). A single cluster
would make all \(z_j=z_0=0\), which is impossible. Hence the five indices
would have to split into two clusters of sizes two and three. The
coefficient sum in the two-element cluster would vanish, contradicting
nonexceptionality.

Therefore all five \(z_j\) are pairwise distinct. By (1.3),

\[
\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal R}(z)\ne0}
\tag{3.3}
\]

at every point at infinity of \(\overline C\).

### 3.3 Normalize to count multiplicities branch by branch

Let \(\pi:\widetilde C\to\overline C\) be the normalization.
The curve \(\widetilde C\) is nonsingular and projective in characteristic
zero. A point of \(\widetilde C\) above an infinity point represents a
particular branch, so singularities and tangencies cause no ambiguity.

Choose a homogeneous linear coordinate \(\ell\) nonzero at that image
point. In the local ring of \(\widetilde C\) define

\[
h=\pi^*(w/\ell),\quad
r=\pi^*(\widehat{\mathcal R}/\ell^{10}),\quad
\delta=\pi^*(\widehat D/\ell^3).
\]

These are regular near the chosen point. The function \(r\) is a unit by
(3.3), and \(h\) vanishes to some positive integer order \(e\). Equation
(3.1) becomes

\[
A h^2r=a\delta^4.
\]

In the discrete valuation ring of the normalized curve, both constants
have order zero, and \(r\) has order zero. Therefore

\[
\boxed{2e=4\operatorname{ord}(\delta).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

It follows that \(e\) is even. This argument applies separately to every
branch above infinity.

Finally, the degree of the pullback hyperplane divisor equals
\(\deg\overline C\). This remains true under the birational normalization.
Equivalently, compare with a generic hyperplane cutting \(\overline C\)
in \(\deg\overline C\) smooth points; on the normalization the ratio of
the two linear forms is a meromorphic function whose zero and pole
divisors have the same degree. Thus

\[
\deg C=\sum_{p\mid\infty}e_p,
\]

which is even by (3.4). This proves the theorem.

**Geometric foundations.** Existence of the nonsingular projective model
and the discrete-valuation description are standard normalization facts
for curves; see the Stacks Project, Section 53.2 (tag 0BXX). Degrees are
preserved under a birational proper curve morphism by Lemma 33.44.4
(tag 0AYU); the degree of a section's zero divisor is treated in Section
33.44 (tag 0AYQ), in particular Lemmas 33.44.9 and 33.44.12. No
point-counting theorem is used in Theorem 3.1.

### 3.4 Equivalent pole-order form

At a normalized branch let the five affine nodes have common maximum pole
order \(e\), and leading values \(z_j\). Their distinctness makes the
resultant have exact pole order \(10e\): the degree-ten leading part has
nonzero coefficient, and every lower-degree part has pole order at most
\(9e\). The identity \(A\mathcal R=aD^4\) then gives

\[
10e=4\,\text{(pole order of }D\text{)}.
\]

Thus \(e\) is even. This agrees with (3.4), since the homogenized norm
\(\delta\) has order \(3e-5e/2=e/2\).

The divisor proof is used rather than assuming that every curve has a
polynomial parametrization. The theorem covers smooth and singular cubic
curves, including nonrational ones, and components with several infinity
points.

## 4. Consequences for the components actually being counted

The theorem proves the following dichotomy.

* For a nonexceptional pattern, every geometric component has even degree.
  In particular, there are no cubic components or any other odd-degree
  components.
* If an odd-degree component occurs, the pattern must satisfy one of the
  four exact minor relations of Section 2. Its pattern belongs to the
  smaller set counted in (2.1).

The statement concerns the algebraic components themselves, not merely
selected integer points. No reconstruction identity beyond the retained
fixed-dividend equation was added to obtain it.

A useful further consequence concerns conics. For a nonexceptional
geometrically irreducible conic, the infinity divisor has degree two and
positive even multiplicities. It is therefore \(2p\) for one point \(p\).
For a conic defined over \(\mathbb Q\), that unique point is rational.
The conic is thus parabolic in its affine plane, with one double point
of intersection with the line at infinity. The five leading node values
there are distinct by Section 3.2. It falls exactly under the retained
parabolic arithmetic lemma.

A nonexceptional \(\mathbb Q\)-irreducible conic cannot split into two
geometric lines, since those would be odd-degree components. More
geometrically reducible components of higher degree can either be counted
using the \(\mathbb Q\)-irreducible curve theorem below or have their
bounded rational intersections counted separately. Degree additivity means
that a \(\mathbb Q\)-irreducible component also has even degree.

After the conics have been treated arithmetically, the smallest remaining
degree for nonexceptional patterns is therefore four.

## 5. Exceptional patterns contribute only the previous harmless term

For completeness, the count (2.1) is elementary. Hodge duality turns a
three-vector with one specified zero maximal coordinate into a decomposable
integral bivector \(w\) in five coordinates with \(w_{45}=0\). Put

\[
\mathbf a=(w_{14},w_{24},w_{34}),\quad
\mathbf b=(w_{15},w_{25},w_{35}),\quad
\mathbf t=(w_{23},-w_{13},w_{12}).
\]

The Pluecker equations give

\[
\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=0,\qquad
\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf a=\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf b=0.
\]

If \(\mathbf a=\mathbf b=0\), there are \(O(X^3)\) choices. Otherwise,
write \(\mathbf a=r\mathbf z\), \(\mathbf b=s\mathbf z\), with
\(\mathbf z\) primitive integral, and \(r,s\) integers not both zero.
Let \(Z=\|\mathbf z\|_\infty\). Then \(1\le Z\ll X\),
\(|r|,|s|\ll X/Z\), and \(\mathbf z\cdot\mathbf t=0\).

For fixed primitive \(\mathbf z\), there are \(O(X^2/Z)\) choices for
\(\mathbf t\). Use a largest coordinate as the modulus \(Z\); the
congruence on the other two coordinates is surjective by primitivity, and
the last coordinate is determined. The bound is uniform since \(Z\ll X\).

On a dyadic \(Z\)-range the number of possibilities is

\[
O(Z^3)\,O((X/Z)^2)\,O(X^2/Z)=O(X^4).
\]

Summing the \(O(\log(2X))\) ranges proves the assertion, including
nonprimitive vectors and every common divisor.

In the main range \(K\ge C_1X\), the retained line-exclusion lemma
removes all actual points on line components, for both exceptional and
nonexceptional patterns. Every remaining component in an exceptional
pattern has degree at least two and at most 24. The uniform curve bound
therefore gives, in the original gap box,

\[
O\left(H^{1/2}\log(2H)\right)
\]

points per pattern, including all its finitely many components. The
bounded state multiplicity changes only the constant. Their combined
contribution is

\[
\boxed{O_\eta\left(a^\eta X^4H^{1/2}\right).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This single term covers the exceptional cubics and all the exceptional
higher-degree components as well as exceptional conics. No claim that an
exceptional pattern actually supports any such component is needed.

## 6. The retained arithmetic contribution from conics

Every nonexceptional conic has just been shown to be parabolic with five
distinct leading node values. The uniform \(O_\eta(a^\eta)\) count for
actual states on each such component is retained from Section 5 of the
135/458 note. To specify exactly what is being used, that argument is:

* If there are more than two actual integer gap points, their affine plane
  has height bounded by a fixed power of \(a\). Completing the square in
  the plane conic gives an integer parameter \(t\) with node polynomials
  \(s_j(t)\) of degree at most two and bounded rational coefficient height.
* The two quartics determine the normalized moment functional up to scale,
  because the eight polynomials \(x^jP,x^jZ\), \(0\le j\le3\), are
  linearly independent when the resultant is nonzero. Actual states have
  nonzero resultant because \(D>0\).
* Consequently \(q(t)\) and \(b(t)\) are rational functions of bounded
  degree and height. Each actual-state normalization is used as a further
  bounded-degree cut unless it holds identically.
* If \(A P_t(-q(t))=aH_4(t)\) holds identically, the monic equation makes
  \(q(t)\) a polynomial. Distinct leading nodes give \(\deg H_4=12\),
  which forces \(\deg q=3\).
* If the quartic remainder relation also holds identically, it writes
  \(b(t)=-aW(t)/V(t)\) with \(\deg W=9\), \(\deg V=12\).
  Thus in lowest terms \(b(t)\) has a nonconstant denominator.
* For coprime integer polynomials \(F,G\) with \(\deg G>0\), integrality
  of \(F(t)/G(t)\) forces \(G(t)\) to divide the nonzero resultant
  \(\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\). Bounded degrees and polynomial height in
  \(a\) give \(O_\eta(a^\eta)\) parameter values via the divisor bound.

All exceptional denominator cases and the finite-cut alternatives remain
as in that lemma; no identity is imposed on the whole conic merely
because it holds at a few actual points. The total number of conics per
pattern is bounded by 24. Summing over \(O(X^5)\) patterns gives

\[
\boxed{O_\eta(a^\eta X^5).}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Unlike the preceding presentation, the current parity argument makes a
separate treatment of nonparabolic conics unnecessary for nonexceptional
patterns. They cannot occur there. Exceptional conics are already included
in (5.1).

## 7. Degree at least four: the quarter-power lattice weight

The coefficient-uniform affine-curve theorem used is

\[
N_{\mathrm{aff}}(C,B)
\ll_n d^3B^{1/d}(\log B+d)
\]

for an affine curve over \(\mathbb Q\), irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\),
of degree \(d\) in fixed ambient dimension \(n\ge2\). This is Corollary
2.2 of Cluckers--Debes--Hendel--Nguyen--Vermeulen, *Improvements on dimension
growth results and effective Hilbert's irreducibility theorem*, Forum of
Mathematics, Sigma 13 (2025), e153, DOI 10.1017/fms.2025.10096. The corollary
attributes the estimate to Castryck--Cluckers--Dittmann--Nguyen (2020).
The implied constant is independent of coefficient height.

In lattice coordinates, a fixed pattern's actual gap vector lies in a box
of radius \(O(H/\lambda_1(L_{\mathbf p}))\). A represented pattern has
\(\lambda_1\le\sqrt5H\); thus a ceiling or a minimum box radius of one
changes only constants. The invertible linear change preserves degrees.
Every remaining nonexceptional curve now has degree at least four. Its
contribution is at most

\[
O\left((H/\lambda_1(L_{\mathbf p}))^{1/4}\log(2H)\right).
\tag{7.1}
\]

To sum this weight, use the retained incidence estimate (1.5). For primitive
lattices of determinant at most \(Y\), the shortest-vector length is
\(O(Y^{1/3})\). A dyadic sum gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{L\text{ primitive}\ \det L\le Y}}
\lambda_1(L)^{-1/4}
&\ll\sum_{R\ll Y^{1/3}}Y^4R^{3-1/4}\\
&\ll Y^{4+11/12}=Y^{59/12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For \(\mathbf p=g\mathbf p_0\), with \(\mathbf p_0\) primitive,
\(\det L_{\mathbf p}=\|\mathbf p_0\|_2\le\sqrt{10}X/g\).
There are two primitive orientations per lattice. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\operatorname{Ht}(\mathbf p)\le X}
\lambda_1(L_{\mathbf p})^{-1/4}
\ll\sum_{g\le X}(X/g)^{59/12}\ll X^{59/12}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

All sums include nonprimitive minor vectors and unusually short lattices.
Enlarging from actual nonexceptional patterns to every decomposable pattern
is harmless. Combining (7.1)--(7.3) and the bounded state multiplicity gives

\[
\boxed{O\left(H^{1/4}X^{59/12}\log(2H)\right).}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Together, (5.1), (6.1), and (7.4) prove (0.1) in the main range.

## 8. Global exponent and the complementary range

The elementary drop bound is retained in the form

\[
Q\ll_\eta a^{1+\eta}/T^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For clarity, the remainder drops in a quotient block have total at most
\(a/Q\), so at least half are at most \(2a/(QT)\). At drop \(d\),
\(a+d=(q_i+1)b_i\), and distinct remainders are divisors of \(a+d\le2a\).
The elementary divisor estimate gives (8.1). The selected good windows
retain at least a fixed positive proportion of the block; small \(T\)
and the finite range of small \(a\) are absorbed into constants.

Substitute

\[
H\asymp Q/T,\qquad X\asymp aQ^2/T^6
\]

into the three terms of (0.1). Absorb logarithms into arbitrarily small
powers of \(a\).

* If the \(X^5\) term dominates, then
  \(T^{31}\ll a^{5+\eta}Q^{10}\), hence
  \(T^{51}\ll a^{15+11\eta}\). This gives exponent \(5/17\).
* If the \(X^4H^{1/2}\) term dominates, then
  \(T^{51}\ll a^{8+2\eta}Q^{17}\), hence
  \(T^{85}\ll a^{25+19\eta}\). This also gives \(5/17\).
* From the \(X^{59/12}H^{1/4}\) term,
  \[
  T^{369}\ll_\eta a^{59+12\eta}Q^{121},
  \]
  and then by (8.1),
  \[
  \boxed{T^{611}\ll_\eta a^{180+133\eta}.}
  \tag{8.2}
  \]

If the line-exclusion scale \(K\ge C_1X\) fails, then

\[
T^{11}\ll a^2Q^3,\qquad
T^{17}\ll_\eta a^{5+3\eta}.
\]

This is again controlled by the smaller exponent \(5/17\), because

\[
180/611-5/17=5/10387>0.
\]

Choose \(\eta\) sufficiently small relative to a prescribed
\(\varepsilon>0\), and sum the \(O(\log a)\) dyadic quotient blocks.
The last logarithm is absorbed into \(a^\varepsilon\). We obtain the
claimed research candidate

\[
\boxed{P(a,b)\ll_\varepsilon a^{180/611+\varepsilon}.}
\]

At the limiting scales,

\[
T\sim a^{180/611},\quad Q\sim a^{251/611},\quad
H\sim a^{71/611},\quad X\sim a^{33/611},\quad K\sim a^{38/611}.
\]

In particular \(K/X\sim a^{5/611}\), consistent with the main-range
line exclusion.

## 9. What the parity argument does and does not give

The theorem excludes odd-degree components for nonexceptional patterns,
not quartic components. It does not justify substituting degree six in the
curve count. Singularities and several branches at infinity have been
included through normalization, so they are not missing exceptions to the
parity statement.

More generally, if the remaining generic components could all be shown to
have degree at least \(d\), the same lattice calculation would give

\[
H^{1/d}X^{5-1/(3d)},
\]

with corresponding exponent

\[
\frac{45d}{153d-1}.
\]

The values \(d=2,3,4\) give \(18/61,135/458,180/611\), respectively.
This expression approaches \(5/17\) from above. The other local terms
and complementary scale also currently have exponent \(5/17\).
Consequently, eliminating higher-degree components one at a time within
this unchanged framework cannot by itself produce an exponent below
\(5/17\). Further progress beyond that threshold needs another saving
in the pattern count, the arithmetic contribution, or the scale analysis.

## 10. Verification performed for this refinement

No independent-agent review or formal verification was performed. The
following exact computations were executed in the current run and are
included in the companion archive.

1. A generic symbolic computation verifies that the substituted norm
   polynomial has degree three and homogeneous part
   \(D_3=\sum c_js_j^3\). Ten generic coefficient-pair identities and the
   homogenized square/fourth-power identity are checked symbolically.
2. Three symbolic Vandermonde determinants are verified. All 52 partitions
   of five indices are enumerated to confirm that a nontrivial collision
   partition without singleton clusters has type \(2+3\).
3. Sixteen explicitly constructed rational node families verify the exact
   degree-ten resultant leading coefficient. They are algebraic test
   families, not claimed to be actual orbit curves. Four pole-scale
   substitutions per family check the same leading coefficient under
   \(t=\tau^{-e}\). These checks do not prove the divisor statement.
4. The retained finite-positive-measure test was rerun at 200 genuine orbit
   starts, each with at least ten positive states. It verifies the
